\section{Introduction}

Automated building-maintenance systems convert photographs into tracked defect
records: a re-photographed defect must eventually be \emph{re-identified},
matched to its record, or given a new one. This paper first studies
within-visit re-identification under viewpoint change. Wall cracks are a
common and difficult case, because the same crack is re-photographed from
different distances, angles, and lighting, and grows between visits.

Crack analysis is a mature \emph{detection} and \emph{segmentation} task, and
its datasets, CrackSeg9k~\cite{kulkarni2022crackseg9k} and
OmniCrack30k~\cite{benz2024omnicrack30k}, carry no cross-observation identity
labels, because the task they serve needs none. We know of no benchmark for
crack \emph{re-identification}; the natural approach, crop-and-match as in
person and vehicle re-ID~\cite{zhou2019osnet,qian2024reidtrends}, assumes a
crack has stable, locally distinctive appearance, and whether it does has
never been measured. We introduce \textsc{CrackID}, 140 photographs, 17
walls, 212 crack identities, to make that re-identification measurement
(Fig.~\ref{fig:ceiling}). Capture is one continuous walk-around per wall, so
what is measured is \emph{within-visit} viewpoint and scale correspondence,
a hypothesized upper bound on multi-visit performance, not a measurement of
it; we add a synthetic revisit
protocol as a controlled secondary check (Sec.~\ref{sec:hybridbench}).
That check uses a distinct \hybrid{} pipeline: it extends the
Table~\ref{tab:main} \ours{} pure registration-plus-Chamfer baseline with a
skeleton-comparison fallback and per-pair blending ($\tau{=}8.0$\,px), so its
reversal does not directly contradict Table~\ref{tab:main}.

\smallskip
\noindent{Appearance buys almost nothing on the decision.} On ranking
the ten zero-shot matchers separate (Rank-1 $0.370$ to $0.613$ on the new
$n_Q{=}305$ rerun; prior chance $0.336$ TBD); on pairwise $F_1$, the \emph{same-crack-or-not} decision a
record makes at a threshold, new rows land between $+0.032$ and $+0.069$ above
the prior $0.305$ best-constant floor ($F_1^{*}$ $0.337$--$0.374$; floors TBD),
and at a
validation-calibrated threshold, the nine new rows cluster at $0.331$--$0.338$.
With no wall-level intervals, no fine ranking is claimed.

Two controls say where the difficulty is not (prior $n_Q{=}280$ snapshot,
pending rerun). Giving an embedder the surrounding wall raises OSNet's open-set rate from $0.168$ to
$0.404$; \emph{erasing the crack} from that view costs almost none of it
($0.393$). Such a method retrieves on the wall, and the cue that works, even
implicitly, is \emph{location}.

A geometric baseline makes location explicit (prior snapshot, not numerically
comparable to the rerun). Registering on non-crack texture, projecting crack masks into a common frame, and
Chamfer-matching under global assignment reaches $\mathrm{DIR}@\mathrm{FAR}{=}0.1$ of $0.768$ against $0.384$ (best new crop, SuperGlue) and
$0.404$ (best prior context), answering $48\%$ of pairs; a \coverage{}
control scores $0.000$ on the same metric, so the open-set gap is matching,
not alignment coverage. This is a conditional, provenance-limited result, not a deployment-ready
system: two test walls have zero registration success, and the annotation
and baseline both use homography-based alignment. The resulting geometric
advantage is therefore a hypothesis about this benchmark, not an
independently established identity effect.

The revisit check reverses the ranking story. The real data are one continuous walk-around per wall, minutes apart, not separate
visits. Under the small-warp synthetic protocol (affine $1.08{\times}$,
$6^{\circ}$, $4^{\circ}$; one seed; five walls), OSNet$+$context
beats the \hybrid{} at R@1 ($0.826/0.671$ versus $0.275/0.274$) and open-set
rate ($0.739/0.663$ versus $0.343/0.342$) for growth/appearance change; the
\hybrid{}'s small assignment-$F_1$ advantage is not evidence that geometry
solves real multi-visit re-identification.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Contributions:} (i)~\textsc{CrackID} with an explicit
annotation-provenance report, $45\%$ of identities formed by an automatic
merge, reported rather than assumed (Sec.~\ref{sec:annotation});
(ii)~a ten-method zero-shot benchmark against stated chance levels, with
crop$+$context and crack-erased controls (Sec.~\ref{sec:ceiling});
(iii)~a small single-seed synthetic revisit checkpoint isolating appearance change from damage
evolution, the only multi-visit evidence this paper has
(Sec.~\ref{sec:hybridbench});
(iv)~a geometric reference baseline (\ours{}) with coverage-aware metrics,
unanswerable queries, and an admission gate, kept distinct from the synthetic
\hybrid{} variant (Sec.~\ref{sec:method}, \ref{sec:protocol}, \ref{sec:results}).
