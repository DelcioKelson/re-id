\section{A Geometric Reference Baseline}
\label{sec:method}

The test examines whether the position of the crack has an effect on the association by aligning the wall surfaces and then comparing the projected masks. It makes the assumption of a roughly planar wall and that there is no \textsc{CrackID} training. This kind of procedure is typical for spatial verification; what is provided here is the protocol, the labels and the controls. The masks (UNet++~\cite{zhou2018unetplusplus,ronneberger2015unet}, ResNet-34~\cite{he2016resnet}, only those that are publicly available, an area of 200~px, with a 5~px closing) are taken as given and the error is not measured.

\subsection{Registration using wall texture}

For $I_a$ and $I_b$, we estimate a homography $H$ (such that $x’\sim Hx$) based on the surrounding wall texture, with the cracks enlarged and then omitted, so the alignment relies on surface features without accounting for the crack under examination. This independence comes from the masks and the following stage, not from each individual pair. The complete detector settings (Scale-Invariant Feature Transform (SIFT)~\cite{lowe2004sift} together with Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalisation (CLAHE)~\cite{zuiderveld1994clahe}, ratio test, robust estimation) are available together with the code.

\subsection{Registration cascade and validity checks}

The textured render produces keypoints, whereas smooth plaster does not. For the cascade, masked SIFT is used; if the number of keypoints is too low, a fallback to unmasked SIFT is employed (in this case it is flagged and crack leakage occurs during alignment); finally, photometric alignment is carried out using the Enhanced Correlation Coefficient (ECC)~\cite{evangelidis2008ecc}. The candidate solutions are tested for plausibility (they must not contain mirrored or folded quads; the scale must be within $[1/16,16]$; there is no criterion based on inlier count here). We record the result of each surviving stage for each pair, and the inlier root-mean-square error (RMSE) at that stage determines the comparison tolerance. The absence of a registration for a pair means a lack of evidence, not that the cracks differ.

\subsection{Crack projection and geometric comparison}
\label{sec:chamfer}

The Accepted Registrations project instances from $I_a$ into $I_b$ so they can be compared using the symmetric mean Chamfer distance~\cite{barrow1977chamfer} as in \eqref{eq:chamfer}; this method allows small misalignments by using intersection over union (IoU) on narrow cracks. Let $a,b$ be crack point sets and $d(p, b)=\min_{q \in b}\lVert p-q\rVert_2$:
\begin{equation}
D(a,b)=\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{1}{|a|}\sum_{p \in a} d(p, b)+
\frac{1}{|b|}\sum_{q \in b} d(q, a)\right].
\label{eq:chamfer}
\end{equation}
We reject candidates that are far away (using distance in the code). Chamfer coverage (i.e., the fraction within the per-pair RMSE, with the distance clamped at [2,16] pixels and a default of 6 pixels) is the scale-free equivalent.

\subsection{Correspondence decisions and limits of spatial evidence}

For each pair of photographs we carry out a one-to-one matching using the Hungarian method~\cite{kuhn1955hungarian} at the validation threshold; pairs that fail to be registered are given a value of $-\infty$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:protocol}). Alignment of an image with an empty wall acts as evidence of the absence of a target; missed detections, areas that are out of frame, and registration failures still remain unresolved. The reference targets do not show any change in damage; performance is determined by the surface and by the origin of the label.
