\section{A Geometric Reference Baseline}
\label{sec:method}

The baseline tests whether crack locations on the wall drive the
association: it aligns wall surfaces to a common frame and then compares
the projected crack masks. It assumes a roughly planar wall and uses no
\textsc{CrackID} training. The shared UNet++ masks (trained on public
crack data only; no \textsc{CrackID} wall appears in their training set)
use instance extraction at 200,px and 5,px to keep inputs consistent
across methods (full segmenter settings are in the code). Masks are taken
as given, and their error is not measured.

\subsection{Registration using wall texture}

For $I_a, I_b$, a homography $H$ ($x’ {\sim} Hx$) is estimated from the
surrounding wall texture with detected cracks dilated and excluded, so
alignment is driven by surface features independently of the identity
under test. This independence is conditional on the masks and the stage
that succeeds, not guaranteed per pair. Full detector settings (SIFT with
CLAHE contrast enhancement~\cite{zuiderveld1994clahe}, ratio test, robust
estimation) are in the code.

\subsection{Registration cascade and validity checks}

Textured render generally provides background features, whereas smooth
plaster does not. A three-stage cascade is employed: first masked SIFT;
if too few keypoints remain, unmasked SIFT as a fallback (flagged
per pair, since the crack then aids alignment); and finally ECC
photometric alignment. Candidates pass plausibility tests (no mirrored or
folded quads; scale within $[1/16, 16]$); the surviving stage is recorded
per pair, and its inlier RMSE sets the comparison tolerance. Unscored
pairs count as lack of evidence, not evidence of different defects.

\subsection{Crack projection and geometric comparison}
\label{sec:chamfer}

Accepted registrations project instances from $I_a$ into $I_b$ for
comparison by symmetric mean Chamfer distance, which tolerates the small
misalignments that would collapse IoU on narrow cracks. Let
$d(p, b) {=} \min_{q \in b} |p {-} q|2$:
\begin{equation}
D(a,b) = \tfrac{1}{2}!\left[
\frac{1}{|a|}\sum{p \in a} d(p, b) ;+;
\frac{1}{|b|}\sum_{q \in b} d(q, a)\right],
\label{eq:chamfer}
\end{equation}
Early rejection of distant candidates applies. Chamfer coverage (the
fraction within tolerance $\tau$ of the per-pair RMSE, clamped to
$[2, 16]$ pixels, default 6 pixels; \hybrid{} uses 8.0 pixels) is
evaluated separately as its scale-free counterpart (see
Sec.~\ref{sec:chamferablation}).

\subsection{Correspondence decisions and limits of spatial evidence}

For each image pair, a one-to-one assignment follows the Hungarian
method~\cite{kuhn1955hungarian} with the validation threshold shared by
all methods; unscored pairs take value $-\infty$ (predicted negative,
ranked last, as in Sec.~\ref{sec:protocol}). An aligned projection onto
visibly empty wall is a \textbf{licensed negative}—evidence of absence,
stronger than low embedding similarity—while missed detections,
out-of-frame areas, and registration failures stay unresolved. The
\ours{} reference targets unchanged damage; progression uses \hybrid{}.
Performance depends on surface-dependent failures and on the
homography-based annotation provenance.
