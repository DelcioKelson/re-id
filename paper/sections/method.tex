\section{A Geometric Reference Baseline}
\label{sec:method}

The baseline tests whether crack location on the wall supports association: it aligns wall surfaces to a common frame and compares projected crack masks. It assumes an approximately planar wall and uses no \textsc{CrackID} training. Shared UNet++ masks (trained on public crack data only; no \textsc{CrackID} wall in training) with $200$\,px / $5$\,px instance extraction keep inputs consistent across methods (full segmenter settings in code). Masks are treated as given; their error is not quantified.

\subsection{Registration using wall texture}

For $I_a,I_b$, a homography $H$ ($x'{\sim}Hx$) is estimated from surrounding wall texture with detected cracks dilated and excluded, so alignment is driven by surface features independently of the identity under test. This independence is conditional on the masks and stage, not guaranteed per pair. Full detector settings (SIFT with CLAHE contrast enhancement~\cite{zuiderveld1994clahe}/ratio/robust estimation) are in code.

\subsection{Registration cascade and validity checks}

Textured render usually supplies background features; smooth plaster may not. A three-stage cascade is used: masked SIFT, unmasked-SIFT fallback if too few keypoints remain (flagged per pair since the crack then aids alignment), and ECC photometric fallback. Candidates pass plausibility checks (no mirrored/folded quads, scale within $[1/16,16]$); the surviving stage is recorded per pair and its inlier RMSE sets the comparison tolerance. Unscored pairs are unresolved lack of evidence, not proof of different defects.

\subsection{Crack projection and geometric comparison}
\label{sec:chamfer}

Accepted registrations project instances from $I_a$ into $I_b$ for comparison by symmetric mean Chamfer distance, which tolerates small misalignments that would collapse IoU on narrow cracks. Let $d(p,b){=}\min_{q\in b}\|p{-}q\|_2$:
\begin{equation}
D(a,b) = \tfrac{1}{2}\!\left[
\frac{1}{|a|}\sum_{p \in a} d(p, b) \;+\;
\frac{1}{|b|}\sum_{q \in b} d(q, a)\right],
\label{eq:chamfer}
\end{equation}
with early rejection of distant candidates. Chamfer coverage (fraction within tolerance $\tau$ from per-pair RMSE, clamped $[2,16]$\,px, default $6$\,px; \hybrid{} fixes $8.0$\,px) is evaluated separately as the scale-free counterpart (Sec.~\ref{sec:chamferablation}).

\subsection{Correspondence decisions and limits of spatial evidence}

Hungarian one-to-one assignment~\cite{kuhn1955hungarian} per image pair plus validation threshold (evaluation, identical for all methods); unscored pairs receive $-\infty$ (predicted negative / rank-last per Sec.~\ref{sec:protocol}). An aligned projection into visibly empty wall is a \textbf{licensed negative}---positive evidence of absence, stronger than low embedding similarity---while missed detections, out-of-frame regions, and registration failures stay unresolved. \ours{} targets unchanged damage; progression uses \hybrid{}. Performance is conditional on surface-dependent failures and homography-based annotation provenance.
