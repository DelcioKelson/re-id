\section{A Geometric Reference Baseline}
\label{sec:method}

The geometric reference baseline investigates whether the spatial location of a crack on its wall can support association with an existing defect record. Different photographs may depict the same damage with substantial changes in viewpoint, scale, and visible context. By aligning the wall surfaces, the method places these observations in a common coordinate frame and compares their crack masks at corresponding locations. In building ticket management, this provides a candidate mechanism for linking a new observation to a previously documented defect.

The baseline assumes an approximately planar wall surface for which image registration provides a useful spatial correspondence. It combines wall registration, crack-mask projection, and geometric comparison, without training a matching model on \textsc{CrackID}. Crack masks are produced by the shared UNet++ segmenter (\texttt{segmentation-models-pytorch}, \texttt{batch\_segment.py} / \texttt{tiled\_inference.py}) and converted into connected-component instances using a minimum area of $200$ pixels and morphological closing of $5$ pixels. The same segmentation and instance extraction are used across the evaluated methods, keeping the input representation consistent.

\subsection{Registration using wall texture}

For photographs $I_a$ and $I_b$, registration estimates a transformation $H$ that maps points from $I_a$ into the coordinate frame of $I_b$: $x' \sim Hx$. A homography models the viewpoint change under the approximately planar-surface assumption. The primary registration stage estimates this transformation from the surrounding wall texture while excluding detected cracks. Crack masks are dilated by $15$ pixels before feature detection, reducing the contribution of crack pixels and their immediate boundaries. This separation is intended to estimate alignment from surface features independently of the crack correspondence being evaluated. It reduces the risk that similar crack structures drive an alignment that subsequently appears to confirm their identity. This independence is conditional on the masks and the registration stage; it is not guaranteed for every image pair. Registration operates on image copies downscaled to a maximum long-side dimension of $1600$ pixels. Contrast is adjusted using CLAHE with a clip limit of $2.0$ and an $8 \times 8$ tile grid. SIFT extracts up to $8000$ features, and FLANN matches descriptors using a Lowe ratio threshold of $0.75$. Robust transformation estimation uses USAC MAGSAC where available, with RANSAC as a fallback, a $4$-pixel reprojection threshold, and up to $10\,000$ iterations. Acceptance requires at least $25$ inliers.

\subsection{Registration cascade and validity checks}

The available alignment evidence depends on the wall surface. Textured render can provide features outside the cracks, whereas smooth painted plaster may contain too little background structure for reliable matching. The baseline therefore uses a three-stage registration cascade. The first stage applies SIFT with crack regions excluded. If masking leaves fewer than $300$ keypoints in either image, a second SIFT stage restores the crack regions. This fallback increases the available feature set, but allows the damage itself to contribute to alignment. Its results must therefore be distinguished from those obtained using masked wall texture. The third stage uses ECC photometric alignment over a $320{\to}640{\to}1280$-pixel pyramid, with translation followed by Euclidean initialisation and up to $300$ iterations per level. An ECC result is accepted only when the enhanced correlation exceeds $0.80$. Candidate transformations are checked for geometric plausibility. Mirrored or folded quadrilaterals, collapsed sides, and area scale factors outside $[1/16,16]$ are rejected. The successful stage---masked SIFT, unmasked SIFT, or ECC---is recorded for each image pair (\texttt{sift}, \texttt{sift-nocrackmask}, \texttt{ecc}). When available, the full-resolution inlier root-mean-square error provides an estimate of registration uncertainty used in the mask-comparison tolerance. If no stage produces an accepted transformation, the image pair remains unscorable. Registration failure indicates insufficient alignment evidence and does not establish that the observations represent different defects.

\subsection{Crack projection and geometric comparison}
\label{sec:chamfer}

Following accepted registration, crack-instance pixels from $I_a$ are projected into the coordinate frame of $I_b$. Each projected mask is compared with candidate instances in the reference photograph. The primary comparison uses symmetric mean Chamfer distance. This measure is less sensitive to small alignment errors than direct mask overlap: because cracks are narrow, a small displacement can substantially reduce intersection-over-union even when two masks depict the same crack. Let $a$ be a projected crack mask, $b$ a candidate reference mask, and $d(p,b) = \min_{q\in b} \|p - q\|_2$ the Euclidean distance from pixel $p$ to the nearest pixel in $b$. The symmetric mean Chamfer distance is
\begin{equation}
D(a,b) = \tfrac{1}{2}\!\left[
\frac{1}{|a|}\sum_{p \in a} d(p, b) \;+\;
\frac{1}{|b|}\sum_{q \in b} d(q, a)\right],
\label{eq:chamfer}
\end{equation}
over the union bounding box, with early rejection of spatially distant candidates. Distances are measured in full-resolution pixels, with smaller values indicating closer geometric agreement.

As a complementary measure, Chamfer coverage evaluates the fraction of mask pixels lying within tolerance $\tau$ of the other mask: $\tfrac{1}{2}(|\{p\in a:d(p,b)\le\tau\}|/|a|+|\{q\in b:d(q,a)\le\tau\}|/|b|)$. The tolerance is derived from each image pair's registration error and clamped to $[2,16]$ pixels. A default of $6$ pixels is used when that error is unavailable (the synthetic \hybrid{} variant fixes $\tau{=}8.0$\,px). Coverage expresses agreement as a proportion, although it remains dependent on the selected spatial tolerance. The two measures are evaluated separately to examine how the comparison rule affects association performance (Sec.~\ref{sec:chamferablation}). Eq.~(\ref{eq:chamfer}) is a per-pair pixel distance whose scale differs per pair, so a single global threshold makes ranking partly measure calibration.

\subsection{Correspondence decisions and limits of spatial evidence}

Correspondences are selected using the Hungarian algorithm~\cite{kuhn1955hungarian} and a validation-calibrated threshold. One-to-one assignment applies within each image pair; multiple observations may belong to the same defect record. The Hungarian solve is part of \emph{evaluation}, applied identically to every method's score matrix. After alignment, geometric agreement supports a match. Missing detections provide only conditional evidence of a non-match, while locations outside the field of view and registration failures remain unresolved. With $I_a$ projected into $I_b$'s frame, the system observes a region visible and empty of the recorded crack---a \textbf{licensed negative}, positive evidence of absence, categorically stronger than the low similarity a crop embedding produces. Unscorable pairs (no registration, or no retained instance after the $200$\,px size filter) receive $-\infty$ and are charged as predicted negative for $F_1$ and rank last for retrieval. \ours{} primarily addresses unchanged damage under varying acquisition conditions. Damage progression is explored separately using \hybrid{}. Results must also account for surface-dependent registration failures and the dataset's use of homography-based annotation.
