\section{A Geometric Reference Baseline}
\label{sec:method}

The baseline tests whether crack locations on the wall drive the association: it aligns wall surfaces to a common frame and then compares the projected crack masks.
It assumes a roughly planar wall and uses no \textsc{CrackID} training.
The shared masks come from a segmentation model trained on public crack data only (no \textsc{CrackID} wall was used in training).
Crack instances are extracted with a minimum area of 200~px and a 5~px closing kernel so that all methods receive consistent inputs.
Masks are taken as given, and their error is not measured.

\subsection{Registration using wall texture}
\label{sec:registration}

For a pair of images $I_a$ and $I_b$, a homography $H$ ($x' \sim Hx$) is estimated from the surrounding wall texture with detected cracks dilated and excluded, so alignment is driven by surface features independently of the identity under test.
This independence is conditional on the masks and on the cascade stage at which registration succeeds, not guaranteed per pair.
Full detector settings (SIFT with CLAHE~\cite{zuiderveld1994clahe}, ratio test, robust estimation) are archived with the code.

\subsection{Registration cascade and validity checks}

Textured render generally provides background features, whereas smooth plaster does not.
The cascade has three stages: masked SIFT; unmasked SIFT if too few keypoints remain (flagged, because crack features can then influence the alignment); and, finally, photometric ECC~\cite{evangelidis2008ecc}.
Candidate solutions are checked for plausibility: the quadrilateral must not be mirrored or folded, and the scale must lie within $[1/16,16]$; no minimum inlier count is imposed.
We record the stage at which registration succeeds per pair, and the inlier RMSE at that stage determines the comparison tolerance.
Absence of registration means lack of evidence, not that the cracks differ.

\subsection{Crack projection and geometric comparison}
\label{sec:chamfer}

Accepted registrations project instances from $I_a$ into $I_b$ and compare them using the symmetric mean Chamfer distance~\cite{barrow1977chamfer} as in~\eqref{eq:chamfer}, which tolerates small misalignments, unlike IoU, which is overly sensitive to them for narrow cracks.
Let $a and b$ be crack point sets and $d(p, b)=\min_{q \in b}\lVert p-q\rVert_2$:
\begin{equation}
D(a,b)=\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{1}{|a|}\sum_{p \in a} d(p, b)+
\frac{1}{|b|}\sum_{q \in b} d(q, a)\right].
\label{eq:chamfer}
\end{equation}
Candidates whose Chamfer distance exceeds the validation threshold are rejected.
A scale-free alternative is the Chamfer coverage, i.e., the fraction of crack points lying within the per-pair RMSE of the other mask; the tolerance is clamped to $[2,16]$~px, with a default of $6$~px.

\subsection{Correspondence decisions and limits of spatial evidence}

For each pair of photographs we carry out one-to-one matching using the Hungarian method~\cite{kuhn1955hungarian} at the validation threshold; unregistered pairs score $-\infty$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:protocol}).
Alignment of an image with an empty wall acts as evidence of absence; missed detections, out-of-frame areas, and registration failures remain unresolved.
The reference targets show no change in damage; performance is determined by the surface and by the origin of the label.
