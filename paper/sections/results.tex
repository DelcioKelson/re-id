\section{Results and Discussion}

\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Main comparison and the open-set gap}

Geometry attains a Rank-1 score of 0.952 as compared to the best crop result of 0.613 (ViT-B/16) and an mAP of 0.886 compared to 0.497 (OSNet), with a coverage of 0.48 rather than 1.00. After coverage has been exceeded, ranking improvements continue but open-set gains do not: at a FAR of 0.1, DIR is 0.768 as against 0.384 (SuperGlue), 0.404 (the best OSNet+ctx1), and 0.000 for coverage (the chance level being 0.038). In the case of hairline cracks, crop similarities almost never reject, and aligned empty projections function as valid negatives. The time cost is 7.19 seconds per query (LoFTR) as opposed to 0.006 seconds per query (OSNet); geometry requires 27.9 seconds per query and 26.0 seconds per pair (these times being those obtained on one workstation only; SuperGlue and LoFTR were timed in a separate run). With regard to RQ2, alignment allows identification beyond coverage, on the condition that we take into account success, surface, and provenance. The point estimates are based on 19 identities.

\subsection{Coverage, failures, and ablations}

\label{sec:coverage}

\label{sec:chamferablation}

Crops achieve 100\% of the pairs while geometry reaches 48\%. Unscored pairs receive a negative score and are assigned the lowest rank. Censoring reduces ranking accuracy. Still, it increases the pairwise $F_{1}$: the scored sets retain 100\% of the positives but only 36.8\% of the negatives (prevalence ranging from 0.180 to 0.373; floor ranging from 0.305 to 0.544). The coverage-only approach yields an $F_{1}$ of 0.544, a mAP of 0.533, and a Rank-1 of 0.515, so almost all of the ranking and pairwise performance comes down to \emph{which pairs align}; the only exception is the open-set lead (0.768 versus 0.000). The failures depend on the wall (zero on two test walls; a per-wall table is provided with the code). Adjacent frames answer all 305 queries, so the ranking is based on a short baseline; the gap sweeps (going from 305 down to 105 at a gap of 3) maintain Rank-1. Registration is determined by sharpness rather than by frame gap (Wald z = 5.11, p = $3.3\times10^{-7}$ versus z = -2.78, p = $5.4\times10^{-3}$); with sharpness at least 10, registration is achieved in 56.1\% of the cases when one wall is removed from the filtered set. Scale-free coverage results in a loss of 0.240 mAP and 0.103 Rank-1 when compared to Chamfer (DIR increased by +0.014). When crack exclusion is disabled, the ranking remains unchanged, but DIR increases by +0.057; masking is retained for independence. ECC contributes approximately 0.03 to Rank-1 and 0.02 to mAP. The total time is 26.0 seconds per pair (7817.3 seconds over 301 pairs; 27.9 seconds per query as given in Table~\ref{tab:main}), which is about three times slower than LoFTR and more than 100 times slower than the cheapest embedding.
