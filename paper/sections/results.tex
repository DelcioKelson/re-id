\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Main comparison and the open-set gap}
On different pair pools, the geometric reference (prior $n_Q{=}280$ snapshot)
changes the operating regime and is not numerically comparable to the new
$n_Q{=}305$ crop rerun:
prior crop Rank-1 $0.654$ (best) against geometric Rank-1 $0.952$, crop mAP $0.520$
against $0.886$, but the pools differ (\emph{scored} $1.00$ vs $0.48$).
Conditioned on coverage, as excess over the \coverage{}
reference, the prior ranking gains are $+0.437$/$+0.353$, while prior pairwise $F_1^{*} =
0.656$ is $+0.112$ over $0.544$, not the $+0.351$ a comparison against the
crop floor would suggest. The open-set result is the one number
coverage does not explain: prior $\mathrm{DIR}@\mathrm{FAR}{=}0.1$ reaches $0.768$
against $0.384$ for the best new crop method (SuperGlue at $n_Q{=}305$), $0.404$ for the best
prior context control, chance $0.038{\pm}0.014$, and exactly $0.000$ for
\coverage{}. Observed: crop methods return a
similarity to the nearest neighbor, which on self-similar hairline cracks
is often high, so they cannot decline. Interpretation: an aligned
pair supports the \emph{licensed negative}:
the query projects into the gallery frame, where its region is visible and
empty. Relative compute does not explain the gap: LoFTR is the
costliest new crop row ($7.19$\,s/query), OSNet the cheapest ($0.006$\,s/query)
in separate per-method runs (crop costs: \texttt{total\_s}$/305$; absolute timings workstation-dependent).

\subsection{Partial coverage, gap structure, and failures}
\label{sec:coverage}
Every crop method scores $100\%$ of valid pairs, the geometric method $48\%$;
Unscorable pairs are charged as predicted negatives. For ranking
that is a penalty: censoring a complete matrix drops Rank-1 $0.422$ to
$0.27$ at $34\%$ coverage in a held-out control (score matrices not committed;
see paper README). For pairwise $F_1$ it is a subsidy: the
scorable subset retains $93.0\%$ of positives and $37.5\%$ of negatives on the current labels with old registration outcomes ($100\%/36.8\%$ prior), so
prevalence rises $0.203$ to $0.387$ and the floor with it, $0.337$ to
$0.558$ (prior: $0.180{\to}0.373$, $0.305{\to}0.544$). \coverage{} scores $0.544$ pairwise $F_1$ on the prior pool ($0.558$ on current labels with old outcomes) and mAP
$0.533$ on the prior pool (both above prior OSNet $0.309$/$0.520$; current-pool rerun of coverage/geometry pending), and Rank-1 $0.515$ prior-pool, by answering every
registered pair without examining a crack. Its signal is registration overlap:
it assigns one constant score to
registered image pairs and $-\infty$ to unregistered pairs, so candidate
images are ranked even though crack pixels are never inspected. Much of the
ranking and pairwise-$F_1$ margin is therefore \emph{which pairs align}; the
open-set metric is the exception and the result we lead with.

Excluding the adjacent frame, then the two and three nearest, leaves Rank-1
flat on the prior pool (OSNet $0.654$ to $0.648$) with excess over chance stable in $0.24$ to
$0.32$: the near-duplicate structure is real but the flat curve alone does not
prove it is irrelevant, because the answerable pool shrinks (prior $280{\to}105$; current $306{\to}124$)
and prevalence shifts with it. It is also important for interpretation: the adjacent frame answers
almost all queries ($100\%$ prior; $95.8\%$ current), so the headline ranking measures short-baseline correspondence
rather than broad temporal re-identification. Registration success is
strongly wall-dependent ($97/301$ pairs, $32.2\%$: walls 07/08/12 $100\%$,
wall 09 $52\%$, wall 11 $83\%$, walls 13-16 between $3\%$ and $29\%$, walls
10/17 at $0\%$). The pairs that register are the densely annotated ones, so
the $96$ registered pairs that carry a scored query--gallery pair hold $48\%$
of the $31\,822$ valid ones --- $160$ valid cells per image pair against
$104$ for a failed pair --- from a third of the available pairs ($46$ of the
$301$ same-wall pairs yield no valid pair at all). A logistic fit over all
$301$ pairs makes the mechanism
plain: sharpness dominates (median $133.8$ registered vs $7.2$ failed,
wall-clustered Wald $z = 5.1$, $p = 3\times10^{-7}$), frame gap far less
($z = -2.8$, $p = 0.005$). The independent-pairs $z$s ($8.2$, $-5.0$)
overstate the contrast because failure is categorical by wall, so we
resample walls, not queries, and the leave-one-wall-out spread in Rank-1 is
large, so no fine ranking among baselines is claimed. One admission gate
(variance-of-Laplacian $\geq 10$) applied identically rejects $21$ of $74$
test captures and nearly doubles registration, $32.2\%$ to $56.1\%$.

\subsection{Ablations and cost}
\label{sec:chamferablation}
Chamfer distance versus coverage. The scale-free coverage score
motivated in Sec.~\ref{sec:method} is not a free improvement: it drops mAP
$0.886$ to $0.646$ and Rank-1 $0.952$ to $0.849$, the largest movement any
change produces; $\mathrm{DIR}$ and $F_1^{@v}$ barely move ($0.768{\to}0.754$,
$0.582{\to}0.556$), so coverage gives
a threshold decision, not a ranking. Masking. Disabling crack
exclusion leaves ranking and pairwise agreement practically unchanged (to
three decimals) but raises $\mathrm{DIR}@\mathrm{FAR}$ $0.768$ to $0.825$:
masking is not what buys the reported accuracy. This is expected when the
test set contains no deliberate case where crack pixels create a spurious
registration: masking buys evidence independence, not observed accuracy on
this collection. We retain masking as the primary configuration because it
keeps the alignment target disjoint from the identity target, and report the
nomask $+0.057$ openly as a safeguard awaiting a harder stress test.
ECC fallback. With ECC enabled (primary): Rank-1 $0.952$, mAP $0.886$,
\emph{scored} $0.48$; with ECC disabled: Rank-1 $0.920$, mAP $0.862$,
\emph{scored} $0.50$. Assignment $F_1$ rises
$0.769$ to $0.784$ when ECC is enabled; open-set DIR is unchanged. ECC was
kept versioned after a fix for a configuration in which it had been inert.

Cost. The geometric pass costs $7817$\,s total on test in our sessions
($27.9$\,s/query if charged per query, about $26$\,s per same-wall image pair
over $301$ pairs). That per-query figure is a charging convention, not an
intrinsic one: registration is per image
\emph{pair}, one
homography serves every crack on a wall, and amortized over the $301$ test
image pairs the same pass runs about $26$\,s/pair, after
which any later crack query on the pair pays only the Chamfer pass, not the
homography. Absolute timings are workstation- and load-dependent and are
reported for relative ordering only; relatively, the pair-level cost is
within an order of magnitude of LoFTR,
a crop method with none of the open-set benefit. No fine ranking by cost
is claimed.

\subsection{A synthetic revisit protocol}
\label{sec:hybridbench}
The operational setting a ticket management backend serves is a \emph{revisited}
crack --- one that already has an open issue ticket --- so
we add a second protocol isolating controlled change. Ten source photographs
across five of the walls (walls 01, 02, 04, 05, 09; a single seed, $0$) are each
synthesized into a set of queries under an identical affine warp ($1.08\times$
scale, $6^{\circ}$ rotation, $4^{\circ}$ tilt; affine approximation, not a
full projective revisit): dataset A additionally grows a single tip of the
largest crack component (length $0.18{\times}\max(16,W/14)$\,px, width
$3$\,px, tip farthest from the component centroid, \texttt{hybrid\_eval.py}),
dataset B corrupts appearance while
leaving structure untouched (Gaussian blur $\sigma{=}1.6$, noise $\sigma{=}14$,
contrast/brightness shift); each synthetic query is ranked against the
wall's real instances only. Unlike the Table~\ref{tab:main} \ours{} system, which is pure
registration plus Chamfer scoring with per-pair RMS tolerance, the
synthetic-protocol \hybrid{} folds a structural skeleton comparison into
the geometric baseline as a fallback plus per-pair blend ($\tau{=}8.0$\,px),
scored by the same
code as OSNet$+$ctx (Table~\ref{tab:hybrid}). \hybrid{} and \ours{} are
therefore different systems; the synthetic rows test generalization under
controlled change, not the Table~\ref{tab:main} configuration. The warp is
deliberately modest (real walk-around tilt reaches p90 $75^{\circ}$), so this
is a checkpoint on small geometric change with controlled photometric or
growth change, not a real revisit.

The geometric side does not transfer its main-benchmark advantage here:
OSNet$+$ctx separates the synthetic queries at R@1 $0.826/0.671$, the \hybrid{}
at $0.275/0.274$, with open-set rates $0.739/0.663$ against $0.343/0.342$.
But the split is metric-dependent, and opposite the ranking gap: on identity
agreement, the \hybrid{} holds its own, with assignment $F_1^{a} = 0.646/0.640$
beating OSNet's $0.631/0.609$, and pairwise $F_1^{@v} = 0.569/0.574$
trailing $0.601/0.594$ by far less than the ranking margin. Both rate
$1.00$ scored, and per assignment logs none was decided by the pure-homography branch
alone ($H\% = 0.0$, the share of assignment cells whose verdict came
straight from the homography with no skeleton fallback or blend, from the
\hybrid{} assignment logs rather than Table~\ref{tab:hybrid}): the
geometric pipeline answers \emph{which crack is which} on pairs appearance
also resolves, but loses the global ranking of a hundred-plus same-wall
candidates at one threshold. These rows are a single-seed checkpoint on five
walls, not a claim: they show the Table~\ref{tab:main} advantage does not
imply multi-visit robustness.
