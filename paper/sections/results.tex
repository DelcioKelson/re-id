\section{Results and Discussion}







\label{sec:results}















\subsection{Main comparison and the open-set gap}







Geometric Rank-1 achieves a score of 0.952, whereas the highest score is 0.613 (which is attained by ViT-B/16), and the mAP is 0.886 as compared to 0.497 (that of OSNet) (the score is 0.48 compared to 1.00). When coverage is measured in terms of the amount exceeding \coverage{}, the ranking improvements remain substantial, even though the open-set figure is one that coverage does not consider: $\mathrm{DIR}@\mathrm{FAR}{=}0.1$ is 0.768 as against 0.384 (SuperGlue), 0.404 (the best result with OSNet$+$ctx1), and 0.000 for \coverage{} itself (chance DIR is 0.038). For self-similar hairlines, the crop similarities almost always lead to non-rejection, while an aligned empty projection yields a valid negative. On cost (LoFTR takes 7.19 seconds whereas OSNet takes 0.006 seconds per query; Reg requires 27.9 seconds per query and 26.0 seconds per image pair; the timings are given only relative to each other and SuperGlue/LoFTR were timed in a separate run). As for RQ2 (the geometric aspect): alignment offers identification capabilities that go beyond what coverage alone can provide, on the condition that success, surface, and provenance are taken into account. All the figures are point estimates based on 19 matchable identities.















\subsection{Partial coverage, gap structure, and failures}







\label{sec:coverage}







Crop methods manage to get 100\% of the pairs, while geometry only achieves 48\%; unscored pairs are given a negative score and are ranked at the end. Censoring has the effect of penalizing the ranking, but it also leads to an improvement in the pairwise $F_1$: the group that is scored keeps 100\% of the positives but only 36.8\% of the negatives (the prevalence increases from 0.180 to 0.373 and the floor rises from 0.305 to 0.544). Since the coverage stays the same for registered pairs in the absence of crack inspection, the coverage is 0.544 for pairwise $F_1$, 0.533 for mAP, and 0.515 for Rank-1: a large part of the ranking and pairwise performance therefore depends on \emph{which pairs align}. The only exception is the open-set lead (0.768 compared to 0.000). The failures are due to the wall (zero per cent in the case of the two test walls; the complete per-wall table is provided in the supplement), so the overall scores vary according to surface. The adjacent frame answers all 305 answerable queries, so the main ranking is based on short-baseline correspondence; the gap sweeps (which decrease from 305 to 105 when the gap is 3) keep Rank-1 unchanged even though the pool is shrinking, rather than meaning that the queries are irrelevant. Registration is based on image sharpness rather than on frame gap (wall-clustered Wald z = 5.11, p = 3.3$\times$10$^{-7}$ versus -2.78, p = 5.4$\times$10$^{-3}$; further details are given in the supplement); using a single sharpness threshold ($\geq$10) increases registration to 56.1\% on the filtered set at the expense of one wall, demonstrating that guidance based on capture quality is more appropriate than full-population coverage.















\subsection{Ablations and cost}







\label{sec:chamferablation}







The cost of using Coverage (scale-free) is $0.240$ mAP and $0.103$ Rank-1 compared to that of Chamfer distance, with DIR remaining almost the same at $+0.014$; the comparison rule affects the ranking rather than the decisions. When crack exclusion is disabled, the ranking and pairwise results are unchanged, but DIR increases by $+0.057$; masking is kept in order to ensure evidence independence, even though observed accuracy is not discussed here. ECC contributes ${\sim}+0.03$ Rank-1 and $+0.02$ mAP with no change to DIR. The time required is $26.0$ seconds per image pair ($7817.3$ seconds over $301$ test pairs, that is $27.9$ seconds per query as shown in Table~\ref{tab:main}; Chamfer-only afterward), which is about $3{\times}$ the time of LoFTR per query and more than two orders of magnitude higher than that of the cheapest embedding; the absolute timings depend on the workstation, and the times for SuperGlue and LoFTR were obtained from a separate run.
