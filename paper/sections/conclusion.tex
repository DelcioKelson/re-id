\section{Conclusion}



We present \textsc{CrackID}, which consists of 140 photographs from 17 walls, along with a protocol for retrieval, deciding whether defects are the same, and performing open-set identification with rejection.



The key results are positive: with regard to (RQ1), ten of the zero-shot crop matchers perform better than random, thus setting a viable baseline for retrieval, while thresholded decisions stay near the level of the best constant baseline and therefore call for a combined assessment of ranking, decisions, and rejection; as for (RQ2), local wall context makes a significant contribution to appearance-based association, and the geometric reference shows how surface alignment and projected crack overlap can assist open-set identification on the 48\% alignable subset; regarding (RQ3), the small synthetic revisit probe makes clear the distinction between within-visit association and multi-visit robustness.



The fact that good retrieval performance alone is not enough to ensure reliable crack association shows the need for both local visual context and geometric alignment to perform re-identification. Because \textsc{CrackID} makes judgments not only on retrieval but also on matching and on rejecting unknown defects, \textsc{CrackID} forms a basis for the development of more reliable visual association methods to support defect tracking in ticket management systems.
