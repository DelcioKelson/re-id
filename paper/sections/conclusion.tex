\section{Conclusion}

We present \textsc{CrackID}, which consists of 140 photographs from 17 walls, along with a protocol for retrieval, deciding whether defects are the same, and performing open-set identification with rejection.

The main result is negative and structural in character:
concerning RQ1, ten zero-shot crop matchers outperform random chance, but when a threshold is used, their performance only just exceeds that of the best constant baseline;
concerning RQ2, the wall context has the biggest effect on the appearance of cracks, and a geometric reference assists with open-set identification in only the 48\% of the alignable subset that has been conditioned on annotation provenance;
as for RQ3, a small synthetic revisit probe reverses the ranking with no sign of multi-visit robustness.

The study focuses on single-visit photographs: the figures are point estimates derived from 19 test identities, grouped by wall and matched using only the labels supplied by one annotator during the visual-association stage.
The next crucial stage is to conduct an acquisition session to run a cross-visit benchmark at the ticket level.
