\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Crack datasets, and where they stop}
Automated crack analysis is overwhelmingly a detection and segmentation
literature~\cite{hasan2025review,perez2024assessment}, and its datasets follow:
CrackTree, CFD, CRACK500, DeepCrack, GAPs and their successors annotate cracks
\emph{within} single images, and the large consolidations merging
them~\cite{kulkarni2022crackseg9k,benz2024omnicrack30k} inherit that scope. None
carries an identity label linking a crack in one photograph to the same physical
crack in another, because segmentation does not need one. They answer \emph{where
is there a crack in this image}, never \emph{is this the crack from last time},
and the distinction is not cosmetic: a detector perfect on every visit still
yields an incoherent defect history if the visits cannot be linked. Multi-view
pipelines aggregate across images of one inspection~\cite{mallios2023multiview}
but establish no instance correspondence, so they cannot support temporal
tracking either.

\subsection{Multi-temporal crack monitoring: the nearest neighbour}
\label{sec:related-temporal}
Structural health monitoring does compare crack images across time, with
machinery close to ours. Parente \emph{et al.}~\cite{parente2022monitoring} track
crack width from repeated captures; Harb \emph{et al.}~\cite{harb2025multitemporal}
use temporal sequences to improve crack \emph{segmentation}; Sun and
Chang~\cite{sun2025perspective} perspective-correct handheld time-lapse images so
evolution can be measured under field distortion. RANSAC-estimated homography is
the standard tool throughout, and it appears in patents too, matching a new
crack image against registered ones via SIFT~\cite{nec2019guide}.

The task is different in a way that matters. Throughout this work the
\emph{identity of the crack is given by construction} --- a fixed camera, a
marked location, or an operator guided back to a previous viewpoint supplies the
correspondence --- and the open question is the crack's width or extent.
Accordingly the metrics are measurement metrics (width, area, spine-length
error) rather than association metrics; the subject is one known crack rather
than a wall of candidates; and no dataset is released on which one association
method could be compared against another. The patent case is telling: its
SIFT-and-homography pipeline exists to guide an operator back to the same camera
pose, which \emph{avoids} the re-identification problem rather than solving it.

What is missing is the decision an automated maintenance record requires: given
a new photograph of a wall carrying several cracks, which recorded identity does
each observation belong to, and which are new? That is an open-set association
problem over a gallery, it has no benchmark, and it is what \textsc{CrackID}
supplies.

\subsection{Object re-identification}
Re-identification matches an instance across
captures~\cite{qian2024reidtrends}. Handcrafted local
descriptors~\cite{lowe2004sift,bay2006surf,rublee2011orb} are efficient and
interpretable but need repeatable, distinctive keypoints --- precisely what
hairline cracks on flat render fail to provide. Learned correspondence replaces
descriptor matching with attention: SuperGlue~\cite{sarlin2020superglue} refines
detected keypoints through a graph network and an optimal matching layer, and
LoFTR~\cite{sun2021loftr} is detector-free and dense. Both are markedly more
robust, and both are pairwise, so cost grows with gallery size. Embedding
methods map a region to a vector~\cite{he2023similarity}: ViT and
DeiT~\cite{dosovitskiy2021vit,touvron2021deit}, CLIP~\cite{radford2021clip},
detector backbones~\cite{xu2024localmatching}, and OSNet~\cite{zhou2019osnet},
which is designed for instance-level discrimination.

Our benchmark evaluates all of these. Our finding is not that any one of them is
weak, but that their differences collapse on the decision metric --- a result
that only becomes visible when the population is broad enough and the metric is
the deployable one rather than a ranking statistic.

\subsection{Damage association in inspection pipelines}
The closest integrated systems come from vehicle-damage assessment. Maiano
\emph{et al.}~\cite{maiano2023antifraud} combine filtering, detection, and
OSNet-based damage re-identification for insurance claims. Commercial vehicle
inspection platforms rely on controlled capture rigs. In both cases association
is posed over cropped damage regions and evaluated where acquisition is
curated. Our results suggest the crop scope is itself the limiting choice, and
that a geometry-first formulation --- available whenever the damaged object is a
rigid surface with texture of its own --- changes what is achievable.

\subsection{Geometric alignment}
Planar registration is classical~\cite{hartley2004mvg}, with RANSAC-family
robust estimation~\cite{fischler1987ransac} and well-understood sensitivity to
control-point configuration~\cite{acuna2019homography}; direct photometric
alignment (ECC) is the standard fallback where keypoints fail. Our contribution
is not a new estimator but the observation that in crack correspondence the
alignment target and the matching target should be \emph{disjoint}: the
homography must be driven by the wall precisely because the crack is what we are
trying to identify. Masking cracks out of detection is what makes the projected
position an independent identity cue rather than a restatement of appearance
similarity.
