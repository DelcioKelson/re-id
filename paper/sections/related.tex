\section{Related Work}

Crack analysis is overwhelmingly focused on detection and
segmentation~\cite{zou2012cracktree,liu2019deepcrack,eisenbach2017gaps}; its
datasets annotate cracks within single images and the large consolidations
merging~\cite{kulkarni2022crackseg9k,benz2024omnicrack30k} inherit that
scope, and multi-view crack pipelines
fuse several photographs of one inspection into a surface or a
segmentation~\cite{benz2025crackstructures} without solving instance
correspondence between them. Structural-health monitoring
does compare cracks across time with machinery close to
ours~\cite{parente2022monitoring,harb2025multitemporal,sun2025perspective,nec2019guide},
but identity is always \emph{given by construction} (fixed camera, marked
location, operator at a viewpoint): the subject is one known crack, the
question of width or extent, and no dataset supports method comparison on the
association decision itself. Those systems therefore solve measurement
after correspondence has already been supplied; they do not decide whether
a newly observed crack is the same physical defect as a gallery crack under
an open-set protocol. Our benchmark targets that missing association
decision, while its real capture scope remains within one walk-around
rather than a true longitudinal study.

Object re-identification~\cite{qian2024reidtrends} with open-set
identification~\cite{liao2014openset} is the formal setting we adopt;
keypoint matching~\cite{lowe2004sift,rublee2011orb}, learned correspondence
(SuperGlue~\cite{sarlin2020superglue}, LoFTR~\cite{sun2021loftr}), and
region embeddings~\cite{dosovitskiy2021vit,touvron2021deit,radford2021clip,zhou2019osnet,oquab2024dinov2}
apply directly to crack crops, and our benchmark measures all of them.
Vehicle-damage assessment already re-identifies damage across claims in
curated settings~\cite{maiano2023antifraud}, a task our open-set protocol
mirrors; the rest of that literature treats damage detection and severity
rating rather than association~\cite{hasan2025review,perez2024assessment,mallios2023multiview}.
Our results suggest the crop
scope is itself the limiting choice. The move our baseline makes, aligning
two captures and then reasoning about what is present in one and absent in
the other, is change
detection~\cite{sakurada2015changedetection,alcantarilla2018street}; planar
registration is
classical~\cite{hartley2004mvg,fischler1987ransac,barath2019magsac} with
photometric alignment (ECC~\cite{evangelidis2008ecc}) as the fallback. Our
contribution is not a new estimator but the observation that the alignment
target and the matching target should be \emph{disjoint}: the homography is
driven by the wall precisely because the crack is what we seek to identify.
