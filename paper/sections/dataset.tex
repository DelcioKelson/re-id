\section{Data Acquisition}
\label{sec:dataset}

\subsection{Acquisition and composition}

The \textsc{CrackID} dataset consists of 140 photographs of 17 exterior walls taken at a single site over four half-days using two consumer handheld devices (Mendeley Data~\cite{ferramenta2026crackid}; Fig.~\ref{fig:dataset-examples}).
The walls are either stippled render (textured) or smooth painted plaster, and the only features that are visible are cracks.
The dataset is divided such that six walls are used for validation (walls 01 to 06; 66 photos, 66 identities) and eleven walls for testing (walls 07 to 17; 74 photos, 146 identities).
Only those identities that appear in at least two photographs are included in the answerable queries (18 validation, 19 test).
We added walls 18 and 19 to the dataset afterward and therefore exclude them from all results.
Each wall was photographed during a continuous walk-around (median 3~s between frames, max 81~s); the data include within-visit viewpoint and scale correspondences, providing a more favorable scenario than multi-visit matching, not a formal upper bound.
Cross-wall search is disallowed, so the wall is given for free.
Counts for the frozen snapshot: validation has 6 walls / 66 photographs / 66 identities (18 matchable, 439 known queries, 74 open-set); test has 11 walls / 74 photographs / 146 identities (19 matchable, 280 known queries, 258 open-set); positive query--gallery pairs: 26,012 validation / 5,722 test; valid query--gallery pairs: 89,118 / 31,822. Of 301 same-wall test photograph pairs, 255 have valid cells and 97 register successfully. Of 31,822 valid test query--gallery pairs, 15,328 fall inside registered pairs (48\%).

\subsection{Annotation, and why it needs auditing}
\label{sec:annotation}

The ORB homography method carries every single click from frame to frame and then combines the fragments to form physical-crack identities; the merging process is the greatest risk (since an incorrect merge can turn a hard negative into a false positive).
In the frozen snapshot, 95 of the 212 identities (45\%; 70 of 146, 48\% in the test case) result from merges, with median bridged gaps of 228~px (test) and 286~px (validation).
One annotator examined all the clicks and sorted the unclaimed points (107 of 1,023 were classified as no crack); agreement was not assessed.
Although single-annotator triage reduces the merge risk, it does not eliminate it. The live dataset must not be combined with the frozen figures without first re-running the benchmark.
