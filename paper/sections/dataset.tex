\section{Data Acquisition}



\label{sec:dataset}



\subsection{Acquisition and composition}



The \textsc{CrackID} pilot dataset consists of 140 photographs of 17 walls taken at a single site over four half-days with two consumer handheld devices, archived at Mendeley Data~\cite{ferramenta2026crackid} (frozen snapshot; commit hash in Sec.~\ref{sec:protocol}); see Fig.~\ref{fig:dataset-examples}. Device, resolution and lighting metadata are not archived per photograph. Walls are stippled render (textured) or smooth plaster (feature-poor); no surface roster is archived, so surface is proxied by registration rate and sharpness (variance of Laplacian at quarter scale): walls with median sharpness $\leq 17$ register at 0.00--0.11, walls with $\geq 62$ at 0.29--1.00. Six walls serve for validation (01--06) and eleven for testing (07--17). The test split has 74 photographs forming 301 same-wall pairs, of which 97 register (rate 0.32; 0.00 on walls 10 and 17 to 1.00 on walls 07, 08, 12). Retrieval uses known queries (19 test identities; 280 queries); open set adds queries with no gallery mate (258 on test). Walls 18--19 are excluded; the live labels directory has since diverged and must not be combined with frozen figures without re-running. Code is at \url{https://github.com/DelcioKelson/re-id} (no code licence stated). Each wall was photographed in a continuous walk-around (median 3~s between frames, max 81~s span), an easier setting than multi-visit matching. Cross-wall search is disallowed, so the wall is given for free.



\subsection{Annotation and its limits}



\label{sec:annotation}



The annotation process begins with a labelling tool that applies Oriented FAST and Rotated BRIEF (ORB)~\cite{rublee2011orb} homography to propagate each click across the different frames and then combines the resulting fragments into crack identities. There is no gap threshold preserved and no written rule for merging; instead, one annotator decided to combine the propagated fragments that were judged to be part of the same physical crack. Merging represents the greatest risk since a wrong merge can cause a clear negative to be changed into a false positive. In the frozen snapshot, 95 out of the 212 identities (45\%; 70 out of 146, 48\% on the test set) are the result of merges, the median bridged gaps being 286~px (in the validation set) and 228~px (in the test set) where recorded. That annotator looked at all the clicks and categorised the unclaimed points (107 out of 1,023 were classified as no crack); agreement was not assessed and no second annotator carried out labelling without the homography overlays. Since the geometric reference is also aligned by means of homography, its benefit might be in measuring the agreement between two homography pipelines and one annotator's merges rather than in determining crack identity (see Sec.~\ref{sec:validity}). All the figures refer to the frozen Mendeley snapshot. The positive pool in the frozen state is almost degenerate in that 5,718 of the 5,722 positive pairs involve a merged identity. A subset of the data that has been labelled independently by a second annotator is required in order to quantify the error due to merging.
