\section{Data Acquisition}
\label{sec:dataset}

\subsection{Acquisition and composition}
The \textsc{CrackID} dataset snapshot contains 140 photographs of 17 exterior walls acquired at one
site over four half-days with two consumer handsets, at $3072\times4080$.
The dataset snapshot is archived at Mendeley Data (\url{https://data.mendeley.com/}).
The walls
span two surface families that behave very differently under keypoint
matching: stippled render (densely textured) and smooth painted plaster,
where the crack is frequently the only structure in frame. Walls split
disjointly into 6 validation walls (66 photographs, 66 identities) and 11
test walls (74 photographs, 146 identities). Statistical power is governed
by identities visible in two or more photographs, since only these form a
query with an answer: 18 in validation, 19 in test. Each wall was captured
in one continuous walk-around (consecutive frames a median of $3$\,s apart),
so the dataset measures viewpoint and scale correspondence within
one visit, a hypothesized upper bound on multi-visit performance, not yet a measurement
of it.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Adjacent-frame retrieval.} $95.8\%$ of $306$ answerable test queries have their nearest correct gallery entry in the
adjacent frame. Ranking therefore rewards short-baseline correspondence;
Sec.~\ref{sec:results} sweeps the exclusion gap ($306{\to}247{\to}188{\to}124$ with floors
$0.337{\to}0.288{\to}0.250{\to}0.204$) and reports that Rank-1 stays flat while the
pool and prevalence change with it, so the sweep characterises robustness,
not proof that duplicates are irrelevant.

\subsection{Annotation, and why it needs auditing}
\label{sec:annotation}
One click point per crack is propagated across consecutive photographs
through an ORB homography and one-to-one assignment; identities whose
components appear to be fragments of one physical crack are then merged. The
merge is where risk concentrates: a wrongly joined merge converts a hard
negative into a free positive and inflates \emph{every} method's mAP
together, so it cannot be caught by comparing methods. We therefore report
its extent rather than assume its correctness: $95/212$ identities
($45\%$) were formed by a merge (48\% on test), bridging a median maximum
gap of $228$ px; the adjusted Rand index against the pre-merge labeling is
$0.080$, meaning the merge restructured the partition rather than refining
it. All annotations were validated by a human: a single annotator placed
and reviewed all 2,095 clicks from homography-propagated prefill until
zero provisional points remained. The triage is complete: every one of the 1,023 clicks no component
claimed was reviewed against the imagery --- 916 sit on visible cracks the
segmenter missed rather than labelling mistakes, leaving 107 clicks
($10.5\%$, 95\% CI $[8.7\%, 12.5\%]$) naming no crack
(dataset\texttt{/}labels\texttt{/}\_unresolved.json). The 2,095 clicks resolve
to 1,051 connected components; ``points'' below means clicks, not components.
Single-annotator;
agreement unmeasured, so the merge risk above is bounded by reporting,
not removed. Counts in this section and Table~\ref{tab:units} refer to the
frozen benchmark snapshot; \texttt{dataset/labels/} has
since been re-edited (walls 18--19 added, audit in progress) and its raw
identity/point totals no longer match exactly.
