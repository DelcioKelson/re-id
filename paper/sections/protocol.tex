\section{Benchmark Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

The benchmark measures the visual association component: given a new crack observation, decide whether it matches a recorded defect or has no match in the reference set. Annotated identities serve as proxies for persistent defect records; we measure association accuracy, not duplicate-ticket reduction or full workflow performance.

\subsection{Task formulation and candidate records}

A \emph{wall} is one physical wall; an \emph{identity} links observations attributed to the same crack (provenance in Sec.~\ref{sec:annotation}). A \emph{query} is one crack instance; its \emph{gallery} holds eligible instances from other photographs of the same wall, assuming the wall is known. Cross-wall search is out of scope. A query is \emph{known} if its gallery contains the same identity, else \emph{unknown}. Each method scores query--gallery pairs for ranking, thresholded decisions, and identification with rejection. A \emph{query--gallery pair} is the pairwise unit; an \emph{image pair} is the registration/assignment unit. The Hungarian one-to-one constraint applies per image pair only; several observations may share one defect record.

Test counts and prevalence are summarised in Table~\ref{tab:units} for the frozen benchmark snapshot ($6$ validation walls wall01--wall06; $11$ test walls wall07--wall17; wall18--wall19 added later are excluded from all numbers). Registration is attempted for all $301$ same-wall test image pairs; $255$ yield scored pairs with validity-mask cells (see Sec.~\ref{sec:coverage}).

\subsection{Unchanged damage under different acquisition conditions}

The primary evaluation uses real single-visit walk-arounds per wall, where morphology is treated as approximately unchanged and variation comes from viewpoint, distance, scale, illumination, and visibility, with same-wall distractors and unknown queries included. Walls are disjoint across validation/test. All methods are used as-is without \textsc{CrackID} fine-tuning on shared masks (Sec.~\ref{sec:method}); thresholds are calibrated on validation and applied to test. This establishes within-visit correspondence only, not cross-visit or progression robustness.

\subsection{Controlled appearance change and damage progression}

A complementary synthetic revisit isolates photometric change from structural growth: ten source photographs from five walls, one seed, same modest affine warp in both conditions. Condition B corrupts appearance (blur, noise, contrast/brightness) with structure intact; condition A extends one tip of the largest component. Full parameters are in code. This compares OSNet with context against \hybrid{} (geometric alignment plus skeleton fallback/blend, $\tau{=}8.0$\,px), which is distinct from the primary \ours{} reference. With one seed and five walls, results are preliminary checkpoints, not longitudinal evidence.

\subsection{Decision rules and handling of unavailable scores}

Pairwise classification accepts a pair at threshold; query-level identification accepts the top gallery instance only at threshold, else rejects; assignment applies the Hungarian maximisation per image block then rejects below the validation threshold. Unscorable pairs are retained: predicted negative for pairwise/assignment $F_1$, rank-last for retrieval. A miss on a known query and a correct rejection on an unknown query are thus distinguished from a supported non-match: registration failure is unresolved and may need review, not proof of a new defect. For assignment $F_1{=}2TP/(2TP{+}FP{+}FN)$, correct accepted assignments are true positives, incorrect accepted ones false positives, unmatched known instances false negatives.

\subsection{Evaluation metrics and reference baselines}

Retrieval over known queries uses Rank-1, Rank-5, mAP. Pairwise decisions use precision/recall/$F_1$, distinguishing oracle $F_1^{*}$ from validation-calibrated $F_1^{@v}$. Assignment uses $F_1^a$. Identification with rejection uses DIR (known accepted-correct) and FAR (unknown accepted); DIR@FAR$=0.1$ is the constrained operating point, distinct from the fixed-threshold result. Coverage is the scored-pair fraction and is reported alongside accuracy. References are random ranking and the best-constant pairwise $F_1$ of $2p/(1{+}p)$ ($0.305$ at test prevalence $0.180$; $0.544$ on the $0.48$ geometric subset; $0.452$ at validation prevalence $0.292$); full chance levels in Table~\ref{tab:main}. Resampling is by wall given shared capture conditions; with 11 test walls and 19 matchable identities we report point estimates only, with wall-level intervals and score-matrix release as future work (Sec.~\ref{sec:validity}).
