\section{Benchmark Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

The benchmark assesses visual association by determining, for a new crack observation, whether it matches one of the recorded defects; since annotated identities serve as a stand-in for permanent defect records, the scores indicate the accuracy of the association rather than the reduction of duplicates at the ticket level.

\subsection{Task formulation and candidate records}

There is one physical wall; an identity connects the different observations of the same crack (Section~\ref{sec:annotation}).
A query refers to one instance of a crack; its gallery includes other instances taken from photos of the same known wall.
Cross-wall search is not allowed.
A query is called known if its gallery contains the identity, otherwise it is unknown (meaning it is a genuinely new defect).
In closed-set retrieval it is assumed that the answer will always be in the gallery and the candidates are only ranked.
Open-set identification, on the other hand, involves modelling the association of tickets: the system has to either link the query to the best existing ticket or reject it and create a new ticket if no gallery entry scores high enough.
Each method evaluates query--gallery pairs for the purposes of ranking, makes thresholded decisions and carries out identification with rejection.
Pairs are the unit for pairwise decisions; queries are the unit for retrieval and for open-set decisions; image pairs are the unit for registration and assignment.
The Hungarian one-to-one constraint is applied only for each image pair.

\subsection{Test conditions, decisions, and metrics}

The primary test involves actual walk-arounds for each wall, assuming that the morphology remains unaltered; the only variations considered are viewpoint, distance, scale, lighting, and visibility, along with same-wall distractors and unknown queries.
The validation and test walls are entirely separate.
All the methods are used exactly as they are on the shared masks without any tuning of \textsc{CrackID} (see Sec.~\ref{sec:setup}); the thresholds determined on the validation set are applied to the test set, considering only within-visit correspondence.
A synthetic check is carried out to distinguish between photometric effects (condition B: blur/noise/contrast with unchanged structure) and growth effects (condition A: one tip extended) using ten photos taken from five walls with a single seed and a common warp (parameters available with the code), comparing OSNet with context to \hybrid{} ($\tau{=}8.0$~px), which is different from \ours{}: this is only a preliminary check.

Decisions: any pairs that exceed the validation-calibrated threshold are accepted; for open-set identification, only the highest-ranking gallery entry above the threshold is accepted for each query, and a query with nothing above threshold is rejected as a new ticket.
For each image block, the Hungarian assignment is used with those below the threshold being dropped.
Unscorable pairs are retained (they are predicted to be negative for $F_1$ and are given the last rank in retrieval); a registration failure is defined as the absence of evidence, not as evidence of a new defect.
The metrics used are Rank-1 and mean Average Precision (mAP) for closed-set ranking; pairwise $F_1$ compared with the oracle $F_1^{*}$ versus the validation $F_1^{@v}$; assignment-$F_1^{a}$ equal to $2\text{TP}/(2\text{TP}+\text{FP}+\text{FN})$; and, for open-set, detection and identification rate (DIR) at false alarm rate (FAR) of 0.1 that is, the fraction of known queries correctly linked when at most 10\% of unknown queries are wrongly linked together with coverage (the fraction that is scored).
The references include random ranking and the best-constant $F_1$ $2p/(1+p)$ (0.305 at a prevalence of 0.180; 0.544 on the 0.48 geometric subset; 0.452 in the validation at 0.292); the full chance levels are given in Table~\ref{tab:main}.
Since there are 11 walls and 19 matchable identities, we provide the point estimates (Sec.~\ref{sec:limitations}).
