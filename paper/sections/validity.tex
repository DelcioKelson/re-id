\section{Validity, Limitations, and Future Work}
\label{sec:validity}

This work establishes a first measurable formulation of within-visit crack
re-identification and a geometric reference baseline. The results are point
estimates on a small, wall-clustered sample with single-annotator labels and
partial registration coverage, so each finding below is paired with the
concrete step that would confirm or extend it.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Scale to multi-wall, multi-visit data.} Collect more walls, surface
families, and annotators to support wall-level confidence intervals and reliable
method ranking. Most importantly, add a second capture session at deliberately
varied distances and lighting to convert the within-visit measurement into a
genuine cross-visit benchmark for ticket management backends.

\item \textbf{Add provenance-free annotation.} All clicks were human-validated by a
single annotator with full triage of unclaimed points, but agreement is unmeasured
and the geometric advantage remains provenance-conditioned. A small subset labeled
entirely without homography propagation, plus released per-pair score matrices for
sensitivity analysis, wall-clustered intervals, and threshold-free AP/AUROC, would
quantify how much of the open-set gap is independent of annotation provenance.

\item \textbf{Close the registration coverage gap.} The baseline answers $48\%$ of pairs ($97/301$ image pairs; $15\,328/31\,822$ valid cells) and scores zero on smooth-plaster walls. Surface-adaptive registration ---
learned local features, direct photometric alignment, or sparse depth from consumer
devices --- would push coverage toward full-set evaluation and further decouple the
geometry pipeline from homography-based provenance.

\item \textbf{Learn from geometry-derived supervision.} The baseline is a non-learned
reference, not a deployment target. Using the registration pipeline as a teacher for
pseudo-labels, or fine-tuning a foundation-model backbone on homography-derived
correspondence, would retain the location-based advantage on pairs registration
cannot answer.

\item \textbf{Evaluate the ticket lifecycle.} Define task-level metrics --- duplicate
rate and missed-new-defect rate --- and evaluate segmentation, re-identification, and
ticket assignment jointly in a closed-loop simulation of real inspection workflows.
\end{enumerate}

Together, these steps turn the present within-visit reference into a deployable
multi-visit re-identification component for operational ticket management.
