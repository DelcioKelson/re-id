\section{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:limitations}

This study presents the first measurable test of re-identifying a crack in a single visit using a geometric reference.
The results are derived from a small number of clustered walls, labels provided by a single annotator, and partial registration coverage.
Each of the points listed here corresponds to a finding together with a step for verifying it.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textit{Scale to more walls and visits.}
Collect data from more walls, different surface types, and additional annotators to improve the confidence intervals and the reliability of the rankings.
Most importantly, include a second session using different distances and lighting conditions to establish a genuine cross-visit benchmark.
\item \textit{Add provenance-free annotation.}
One annotator reviewed all clicks and handled the unclaimed points; agreement was not assessed, and the geometric advantage depends on provenance.
To detect the gap independent of provenance, create a small set of labels without homography and share score matrices per pair for sensitivity to wall-clustered intervals and threshold-free AP/AUROC.
\item \textit{Close the coverage gap.}
The baseline covers 48\% of the pairs (97 of 301 image pairs; 15,328 of 31,822 valid cells) and is ineffective on smooth plaster.
By using surface-adaptive registration such as learned local features, direct photometric alignment, or sparse depth from consumer devices, a more thorough evaluation could be achieved, and separate geometry could be better distinguished from homography-based provenance.
\item \textit{Learn from geometric supervision.}
The baseline is an untrained reference and not a system that has been put into operation.
The location benefit could be obtained by using pseudo-labels from the registration process or by fine-tuning a foundation model on homography correspondences, without per-pair registration.
\item \textit{Assess the ticket lifecycle.}
Define task metrics such as the duplicate rate and the missed-new-defect rate, and also test segmentation, re-identification, and joint ticket assignment in a closed-loop simulation of real inspections.
\end{enumerate}
Together, these steps will turn the within-visit reference into a multi-visit re-identification component that can be used operationally for tickets.
