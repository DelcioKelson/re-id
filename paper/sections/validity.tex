\section{Limitations and Future Work}

\label{sec:validity}

This study presents the first measurable test of re-identifying a crack in a single visit using a geometric reference. The results are derived from a small number of clustered walls, labels provided by a single annotator, and partial registration coverage. Each of the points listed here corresponds to a finding together with a step for verifying it.

\begin{enumerate}

\item \textbf{Scale to more walls and visits.} Collect data from more walls, different surface types, and additional annotators to improve the confidence intervals and the reliability of the rankings. Most importantly, include a second session using different distances and lighting conditions to establish a genuine cross-visit benchmark.

\item \textbf{Add provenance-free annotation.} One annotator reviewed all clicks and handled the unclaimed points; agreement was not assessed, and the geometric advantage depends on provenance. To detect the gap independent of provenance, it would help to create a small set of labels without homography and share score matrices per pair for sensitivity to wall-clustered intervals and threshold-free AP/AUROC.

\item \textbf{Close the coverage gap.} The baseline covers 48\% of the pairs (97 out of 301 image pairs; 15,328 out of 31,822 valid cells) and is ineffective when dealing with smooth plaster. By using surface-adaptive registration methods such as learned local features, direct photometric alignment, or sparse depth obtained from consumer devices, a more thorough evaluation could be achieved, and the separate geometry could be better distinguished from the homography-based provenance.

\item \textbf{Learn from geometric supervision.} The baseline is an untrained reference and not a system that has been put into operation. The location benefit could be obtained by using pseudo-labels from the registration process or by fine-tuning a foundation model on homography correspondences, without per-pair registration.

\item \textbf{Assess the ticket lifecycle.} Define task metrics such as the duplicate rate and the missed-new-defect rate, and also test segmentation, re-identification, and joint ticket assignment in a closed-loop simulation of real inspections.

\end{enumerate}

Together, these steps will turn the within-visit reference into a multi-visit re-identification component that can be used operationally for tickets.
