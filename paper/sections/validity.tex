\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:validity}

We state the limits in the body because they bound the central claims.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{The effective sample is 19 identities.} The test split's $280$
queries come from $19$ identities visible in two or more photographs across $11$
walls; queries from one wall share material, illumination and one camera pass, so
query count is not sample size. We resample walls and report the
leave-one-wall-out spread in Rank-1. The same resampling discipline applies to
the open-set headline: $\mathrm{DIR}@\mathrm{FAR}{=}0.1$ of $0.768$ is a point
estimate at one operating point on the same sample, so the abstract compares it
to baselines as a one-number structure --- the $0.35$ gap --- and claims
nothing about the precise spread between resampled walls. This
benchmark supports a large structural claim --- a $0.35$ open-set gap, a
formulation change that lifts a band nine methods sit inside --- but not
fine-grained ranking among baselines.

\smallskip
We do not attach a numeric wall-resampled interval to the abstract point
estimates because the score matrices needed to recompute it are not part of the
released artefacts; the effective sample and wall-level bimodality should
therefore be read as a limitation, not as hidden precision.

\noindent\textbf{Scope is controlled, and the control found something.} The
method reads full photographs; the baselines read crops --- dictated by design,
since homography needs wall context. Two controls
(Sec.~\ref{sec:controls}) separate identity cue from field of view, and scope
does not account for the open-set gap ($0.404$ best context against $0.768$).
But the crack-erased control is uncomfortable: inpainting the crack costs only
$0.011$ of that $0.404$, so a context-scope number is partly background-matching,
which we expect to weaken on real revisits. Ranking numbers are additionally
near-duplicate (the adjacent frame answers all $280$ queries,
Sec.~\ref{sec:dataset}); the gap sweep shows the trend is flat, with power
falling to $105$ answerable queries by gap $3$.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Annotation and the geometric baseline share a mechanism.}
Identities were seeded by propagating one click point between consecutive
photographs through an ORB homography (Sec.~\ref{sec:annotation}), and the
geometric baseline's whole mechanism is homography registration --- so the
ground truth could be biased toward exactly the pairs \ours{} can answer. This
is distinct from the merge-rate disclosure of Sec.~\ref{sec:annotation}: that
reports a deliberate design choice, this would be a measurement artefact. On
this data the entanglement is real but not separable: of the $118$ positive
(same-crack) query--gallery pairs on test, $116$ ($98.3\%$) involve an identity
that was itself formed by a merge, and both-sides-unmerged positives number just
$2$, so a $\mathrm{DIR}@\mathrm{FAR}$ restricted to unmerged identities carries
no statistical power. Two partial checks mitigate it. First, the mechanism is
not exactly shared: annotation warped one ORB homography between
\emph{consecutive} frames, while the baseline registers \emph{every} pair with a
crack-masked SIFT/MAGSAC/ECC cascade and scores by Chamfer --- a strictly harder
alignment problem. Second, positives register at the same rate whether or not
they involve a merged identity ($100\%$ both ways here), so any bias would have
to enter through negatives and the decision threshold, not through positive
coverage. This check does not test whether a biased homography registers
differently from an unbiased one, because both the annotation and baseline
alignment are related procedures; nor does it create a clean positive
subsample. We report the structure rather than assert freedom from it; a
human-audited identification split, with alignment-independent labels, is the
clean experiment that would settle it.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Annotations are instrumented, not audited.} The human verdicts
that would turn the provenance of Sec.~\ref{sec:annotation} into a sampled error
rate with a confidence interval have not been collected; the $45\%$ merge rate is
reported, and relative comparison is robust to it since the merge policy hits all
methods identically. All captures come from one site over four half-days, two
handsets and two surface families; smooth plaster produces the categorical SIFT
failure noted in Sec.~\ref{sec:coverage}; cracks are located by a learned
segmenter whose errors propagate into Chamfer agreement unquantified. What would
convert the cross-visit claim from inferred to designed --- and the synthetic
revisits of Sec.~\ref{sec:hybridbench} to measurement --- is a real second
capture session at marked distances and angles; we have not run one, and every
multi-visit statement here is a hypothesis, not a result.
