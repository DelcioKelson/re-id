\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:validity}

We state the limits in the body rather than a closing paragraph, because two of
them bound the central claim.

\subsection{Scope is controlled, and the control found something}
The proposed method reads full photographs; the baselines read crops. That
follows from the design --- homography estimation needs wall-level context a
crop cannot supply --- but it means a bare Table~\ref{tab:main} would vary
\emph{two} things at once: identity cue (appearance versus geometry) and input
scope (crop versus full image).

Sec.~\ref{sec:controls} runs the two controls that separate them. Scope does
not account for the gap: the best crop$+$context condition reaches
$\mathrm{DIR}@\mathrm{FAR}{=}0.1$ of $0.404$ against the geometric method's
$0.768$. But the crack-erased control returns an uncomfortable result we report
rather than bury --- inpainting the crack out of a crop$+$context view costs
only $0.011$ of that $0.404$. Within a single walk-around the surrounding wall
texture is itself a location fingerprint, so a context-scope number on
\textsc{CrackID} is substantially a background-matching number. We expect this
to weaken across genuine revisits, where surrounding texture also changes,
which is a further reason the cross-visit capture in Sec.~\ref{sec:next}
matters. The controls were run on OSNet and CLIP only, not on all ten
baselines.

\subsection{Two metric properties that are easy to get wrong}
Pairwise $F_1$ has a floor of $2p/(1+p)$ and that floor is a property of the
\emph{pool}, not of the task: it is $0.305$ on the full test pool and $0.544$ on
the subset the geometric method can score, because registration coverage is not
independent of the label --- two photographs register when they overlap, which
is when the answer is present, so the scorable subset retains $100\%$ of
positive pairs and $36.8\%$ of negatives. We therefore charge unscorable pairs
rather than dropping them, and report the \textsc{Coverage-only} control
(Sec.~\ref{sec:reference}) so that the geometric method's pairwise $F_1$ is
read against $0.544$ rather than against the crop methods' $0.305$. On that
reading its pairwise-$F_1$ advantage is modest and its open-set advantage is
not: \textsc{Coverage-only} scores $0.544$ on pairwise $F_1$ and $0.000$ on
$\mathrm{DIR}@\mathrm{FAR}$.

We flag the consequence for anyone extending the benchmark: a new method's
ranking and pairwise-$F_1$ numbers are only comparable to ours at the same
coverage, whereas $\mathrm{DIR}@\mathrm{FAR}$ is robust to it.

\subsection{Every ranking number is a near-duplicate result}
For $100\%$ of answerable test queries the nearest correct answer is the
adjacent frame (Sec.~\ref{sec:scope}). Table~\ref{tab:main} is reported at
\texttt{min\_frame\_gap}$=0$ and is therefore near-duplicate retrieval;
Sec.~\ref{sec:gapsweep} sweeps the gap, but power falls to $105$ answerable
queries by gap $3$, so the sweep constrains the trend rather than establishing
a new operating point.

\subsection{The effective sample is 19 identities}
The test split's $280$ query instances come from $19$ identities visible in two
or more photographs, over $11$ walls. Query count is not sample size: queries
from one wall share material, illumination, and one camera pass. Hence we
resample walls and report the leave-one-wall-out spread ($\pm 0.33$ Rank-1 for
\textsc{CrackShape}).

This benchmark can therefore support a large structural claim --- a $0.35$ gap
in $\mathrm{DIR}@\mathrm{FAR}$, a formulation that leaves a band ten methods sit
inside --- but not fine-grained ranking among baselines. Differences of a few
points between two crop methods in Table~\ref{tab:main} should be read as
uninformative.

\subsection{The annotation audit is instrumented but not completed}
The human verdicts that would turn the provenance of Sec.~\ref{sec:annotation}
into a sampled error rate with a confidence interval have not been collected,
and no second annotator has labelled a subset. Until both exist, every mAP figure
here --- ours and the baselines' alike --- is conditional on the merge policy
being sound. Relative comparison is more robust than absolute level, since the
merge affects all methods identically, and the appearance ceiling is a
comparison among methods rather than an absolute claim.

\subsection{Segmentation quality is not reported}
Crack masks come from a learned segmenter and are treated as given. Mask errors
propagate into instance extraction and hence Chamfer agreement, and we do not
quantify that path: hand-drawn reference masks do not exist for this data. The
released code contains a sensitivity sweep over minimum-area and closing
parameters, checkable once reference masks are drawn.

\subsection{Site, surface, and device scope}
All captures come from a single site over four half-days, two handsets, and two
surface families. We claim a benchmark and a baseline for these conditions, not
a general solution. The baseline assumes a near-planar surface with texture that
is not the crack, and should degrade on curved or featureless surfaces, and
where the surrounding texture itself changes between visits.

\subsection{The measurement that would change the most}
\label{sec:next}
One thing would convert the central claim from inferred to designed: a second
capture session, weeks later, at marked distances and angles. It would make the
protocol cross-visit rather than viewpoint-only, supply exact rather than
estimated viewpoint ground truth, and test the condition under which we expect
geometry to widen its advantage --- appearance drift with position preserved.
That data does not exist yet, and until it does, every sentence in this
subsection is a hypothesis, not a result.

Fig.~\ref{fig:evolution} makes that hypothesis concrete rather than measuring
it: if the same crack is re-photographed after it has grown, does the
identity survive? Reading the panels as a growth sequence (left to right) is
deliberate --- cracks lengthen, they do not shorten --- but nothing about how
this figure was produced constitutes evidence either way. The edit changes
exactly one thing, crack length, and holds everything a matcher could key on
constant: same camera, same lighting, same wall, same JPEG noise. A number
computed by running this figure through \texttt{benchmark.py} would therefore
not measure robustness to evolution; it would measure whether a method can
match a photograph against a clone of itself with part of the annotation
erased, and the identity label would already be known to be correct by
construction. We do not enter these images in the benchmark, and would flag as
suspect any version of this paper that reports them as a retrieval result.

\paragraph{Qualitative diagnostic, not benchmark evidence.}
We nevertheless use the 17 separately disclosed GIMP edits in
\texttt{illustrative\_synthetic/} as a mechanism check, isolated from the
dataset, splits, threshold calibration, and every table in this paper. For
each source/edit pair, we derive a shortened-mask proxy by truncating the
source component at the manifest's disclosed end of the feathered taper; this
is \emph{not} a segmentation result on the edited JPEG.

The scorer we run against OSNet-with-context answers a question a crop
embedding cannot: \emph{what fraction of the structural features of skeleton
A is explained by skeleton B?} Each mask is skeletonised and its centreline
normalised for translation, scale, and rotation, and the reply is a percentage
assembled from four matched feature families --- endpoint correspondence,
branch/junction correspondence, curvature/shape agreement, and topology and
width (degree histogram, tortuosity, segment-length quartiles, median width),
weighted roughly $(0.20,0.25,0.40,0.15)$ and confined to $[0,1]$. Learned
correspondence (LoFTR) may add up to $15\%$ corroborating evidence, but only
matches landing inside a dilated neighbourhood of \emph{both} skeletons and
surviving a RANSAC consistency check count, so the estimate cannot borrow
wall-background agreement the way a context crop can
(Sec.~\ref{sec:controls}); the pure structural readout is reported alongside.

That percentage is not a separate opinion from the embedding. We place both
scores on a common $[0,1]$ scale to state this precisely: the structural score
is already a fraction, and we map the OSNet-with-ctx cosine in $[-1,1]$ with
the affine stretch $(1+\cos)/2$, a monotone map that leaves ordering and rank
correlations unchanged. Across the 17 edits the reserves become comparable:
medians $0.860$ (structural) and $0.968$ (OSNet); means $0.816$ and $0.944$
($r=0.872$), and LoFTR shifts the structural mean by only $0.3$\,pts. The two
do not agree on absolute level --- the embedding sits near the top of the
scale on near-clones while the structural score reserves more headroom ---
but they do agree on ordering, which is the claim the correlation makes.
Where they part is decomposability. Branch/junction
agreement is the most robust family (median $100\%$) --- shortening removes a
terminal arm, so the junction structure of the retained part survives ---
while topology and width is the family the edit damages most on average
(median $76.3\%$), and endpoint agreement is preserved in most edits yet
exactly $0$ where the truncated tip \emph{was} the endpoint (wall04, wall17).
Each of the three strongest structural losses therefore traces to a nameable
break rather than a uniform slide: wall04 ($0.364$; OSNet $0.823$) loses an
endpoint and its topology together, wall17 ($0.400$; $0.826$) loses an
endpoint, and wall12 ($0.494$; $0.908$) loses a junction. This is the
diagnostic a ranked list of cosine similarities cannot give an end-user.

LoFTR supplies at least four skeleton-adjacent correspondences with a RANSAC
inlier in $9/17$ edits; its absence in the remaining edits is treated as
missing corroboration, not negative evidence. These scores describe
consistency under an artificial, near-clone edit only. They do not establish
robustness to growth, camera, illumination, or time, and are not comparable to
the benchmark retrieval metrics.

\paragraph{Controlled discrimination: structural edit plus genuine viewpoint.}
The near-clone diagnostic has two limitations: every pair is positive (neither
method can be wrong), and the gallery is absent (there are no hard negatives).
A combined experiment corrects both. For each source photograph,
\texttt{edited\_viewpoint\_eval.py} creates a synthetic query by (i)~shortening
the crack mask along its principal axis by a chosen fraction $k$ (skeletonise,
project onto the PCA axis, remove the bottom $k$ of the projection range,
dilate the remaining skeleton back to a mask), (ii)~inpainting the removed
crack region with wall texture so the image is photometrically plausible, and
(iii)~warping the edited image and mask by a known affine viewpoint transform.
The query is scored against the real gallery of same-wall photographs using the
standard \texttt{reid\_eval} protocol, so Rank-1, mAP, $\mathrm{DIR}@\mathrm{FAR}$,
and pairwise $F_1$ are computed in identical units for both methods. The gallery
contains hard negatives (other cracks on the same wall under the same material
and illumination), and $\mathrm{DIR}@\mathrm{FAR}$ penalises false alarms on
unanswerable queries. Sweeping the edit fraction $k \in \{0, 0.25, 0.50, 0.75\}$
traces each method's discrimination degradation as the structural loss
increases, and we read the curve at two named operating points: the
no-viewpoint bin ($k$ only, isolating the structural change from geometry) and
the maximum-viewpoint bin of the sweep ($k$ plus the strongest synthesized
deformation), so the structural-edit effect and the structural+geometry effect
are separately observable. Because the edit is parametric and the viewpoint
transform is exact, the resulting degradation curve is reproducible and directly
comparable between methods --- unlike the near-clone diagnostic, which measures
closure rather than discrimination. This experiment is not a benchmark entry: it
uses synthetic queries derived from real photographs, not independent revisits,
and its numbers enter no table in this paper. Its purpose is to test whether the
two competing predictions (Sec.~\ref{sec:next}) --- appearance fragility versus
geometric stability under structural change --- diverge when the comparison is
in the same retrieval units with real negatives present.

Because the discrimination protocol is generic, the 17 GIMP near-clones join it
as real queries: their masks are reconstructed from the disclosed manifest, they
carry the source component's label points, and each is scored once at its
disclosed edit fraction ($1-\text{split\_frac\_kept} \approx 0.55$) with no
viewpoint change, under the identical \texttt{reid\_eval} protocol as the
parametric bins. They therefore land on the same degradation curve at the
no-viewpoint operating point, giving the hand-made GIMP edit a measured
position next to the parametric edits of similar fraction (\texttt{rows}
labelled illustrative in the JSON); being near-clones of a real photograph, they
are exactly as informative as the parametric rows and exactly as non-benchmark.
This is deliberate: the figure in \texttt{illustrative\_synthetic/} remains a
mechanism check for the mechanism's own sake, while the discrimination table
gives it a quantitative, hard-negative context.

What Fig.~\ref{fig:evolution} is useful for is stating the two competing
predictions precisely enough that the real experiment could adjudicate them.
An appearance embedding is trained to be invariant to exactly the kind of
local change the crack itself undergoes as it grows, which argues for
fragility --- but it is also reading the crop-plus-context wall texture
(Sec.~\ref{sec:controls}) that a genuine revisit would perturb far more than
this edit does, which argues the opposite. The geometric baseline's position
estimate should be stable under crack growth by construction, since growth
happens \emph{after} the wall-texture alignment it depends on --- provided
registration coverage itself does not degrade over the longer, weather-exposed
gap a real revisit spans, which is a separate open question this figure says
nothing about. Both predictions are plausible from the mechanism description
alone; neither is supported by anything in this subsection until the second
capture session exists to test them.
