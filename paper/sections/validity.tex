\section{Limitations and Future Work}



\label{sec:validity}



To our knowledge, this study is the first measurable single-visit crack re-ID test with a geometric reference. Threats: (i) 19 identities on 11 walls, queries not independent, CIs wide; (ii) labels and winner share homography, possible circularity; (iii) different pools with label-informative gate; (iv) single-threshold $F_1$ at 0.180 near-degenerate with validation/test mismatch; (v) singleton unknowns, unstratified variation, wall given but location, Building Information Modelling (BIM), physical markers, and Structure from Motion (SfM) are unevaluated.



\begin{enumerate}

\item \textbf{More walls and visits.} Add walls, surfaces, annotators (CIs, paired tests); add a second session for cross-visit;

\item \textbf{Labels without homography.} Small set without overlays ($\kappa$, ARI), re-run all methods, merged-vs-unmerged, plus matrices for CIs and AUROC/AUPRC.

\item \textbf{Registration coverage.} The reference registers 97 out of 301 of the same-wall test photograph pairs (32\% of the photograph pairs; 48\% of the 15,328 valid query/gallery pairs within those pairs) but does not work on smooth-plaster walls, since the median sharpness in such cases is low (see Section~\ref{sec:dataset}). Coverage could improve with surface-adaptive, photometric, or depth-based registration, which would also separate geometry and labelling; for the FAR measure to assess novelty, matched unknowns from the holdout set are required.

\item \textbf{Tickets.} No ticket-level duplicate rate is measured, and the problem that is causing concern is stated without referring to any ticket data. The definitions of duplicate rate and missed-new rate should be established, and the segmentation and assignment processes should be tested in a closed loop.

\end{enumerate}



Together, these turn the within-visit reference into a multi-visit ticket component.
