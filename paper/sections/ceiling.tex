\subsection{Setup}
Ten crop-scope matchers run under an identical protocol, identical inputs,
and identical scoring: handcrafted keypoints (SIFT~\cite{lowe2004sift},
ORB~\cite{rublee2011orb}), learned correspondence
(SuperGlue~\cite{sarlin2020superglue} indoor weights, LoFTR~\cite{sun2021loftr}
indoor weights, both at $512$\,px long side), general
embeddings (ViT-B/16~\cite{dosovitskiy2021vit}, DeiT-S~\cite{touvron2021deit},
CLIP ViT-B/32~\cite{radford2021clip} \texttt{openai/clip-vit-base-patch32},
DINOv2~\cite{oquab2024dinov2} ViT-S/14, YOLOv8~\cite{jocher2023ultralytics}
\texttt{yolov8n.pt} layer $9$), and a re-identification
architecture (OSNet~\cite{zhou2019osnet} \texttt{osnet\_x1\_0} default
weights). All image backbones are zero-shot on \textsc{CrackID} with
ImageNet or web pretraining only; no backbone is fine-tuned on cracks.
Embedding crops are letterboxed to $224{\times}224$ with ImageNet
normalisation and scored by cosine similarity; keypoint and learned
correspondence scores are RANSAC inlier counts under the same validity mask.
LightGlue~\cite{lindenberger2023lightglue} and NetVLAD~\cite{arandjelovic2016netvlad}
are cited as natural stronger retrieval front-ends but are not benchmarked
here; they remain future baselines given compute and scope limits.

\subsection{Result: appearance buys almost nothing on the decision}
On the decision a record actually makes, same crack or not, at a threshold,
no new crop row clears the prior best-constant floor by more than $0.069$
(excess over the prior $0.305$
floor at the oracle threshold, Table~\ref{tab:main}: new $F_1^{*}$ $0.337$ to $0.374$,
i.e.\ $+0.032$ to $+0.069$; floors TBD pending rerun on the $n_Q{=}305$ pool),
spanning handcrafted
keypoints to foundation-model embeddings and more than two orders of relative
compute ($0.006$\,s to $7.19$\,s per query in separate per-method runs,
Table~\ref{tab:main}; absolute timings are workstation-dependent). At a
deployable (validation-calibrated) threshold, the nine new rows cluster at
$F_1^{@v}{=}0.331$--$0.338$ (prior floor $+0.026$ to $+0.033$); no method holds
the prior $+0.046$ LoFTR exception under the new pool, floors TBD. Ranking does
not transfer to deciding: the nine new rows separate over a $0.243$ range on Rank-1
($0.370$--$0.613$),
yet ViT takes best Rank-1 ($0.613$) while its oracle pairwise $F_1^{*}$
sits only $0.039$ above the prior floor, and SuperGlue (seventh on Rank-1) has the
best new crop open-set rate ($0.384$).
Ordering a gallery well and keeping a record are different capabilities. The
collapse after validation calibration is consistent with threshold
instability under a small, wall-clustered sample: methods can order
candidates differently while their score distributions do not support a
stable same-crack/not-same-crack boundary on held-out walls. This is an
interpretation of the observed pattern, not a claim that calibration itself
is the sole cause. With $19$ matchable test identities and no wall-level
intervals, no fine ranking among crop baselines is claimed.

One contributor is checkable from Table~\ref{tab:units} alone.
Validation pair prevalence is $0.292$ ($26\,012/89\,118$) against $0.180$ on
test, so the $F_1$-optimal threshold is not a property of a method but of the
pool it was fitted on: the same scores that separate cracks on the denser
validation pool place their boundary in the wrong place once prevalence
falls, and every method shifts by the same amount. The pairwise-$F_1$ floor moves
with it, from $0.452$ to $0.305$. This is a mechanism, not a measurement: the
threshold-free version of the claim (pairwise average precision, or AUROC
against prevalence) would settle it, and we do not report it; releasing the
saved score matrices it requires is listed as future work
(Sec.~\ref{sec:validity}).

\subsection{Is it the crack, or the wall behind it?}
\label{sec:controls}
Two controls separate identity cue from field of view at fixed backbone: a \emph{crop$+$context}
condition (\texttt{@ctx1}: instance bounding box expanded by $1.0{\times}$
its size on each side) and a \emph{crack-erased} condition
(\texttt{@ctx1@nocrack}: the same widened view with the crack mask dilated by
$5$\,px and filled by Telea inpainting, radius $3$\,px, so the silhouette is
removed rather than blacked out). Context helps a lot: OSNet's Rank-1
rises from $0.654$ to $0.775$, mAP to $0.595$, open-set rate from $0.168$ to
$0.404$, and its pairwise $F_1^{*}$ from $0.309$ to $0.354$ (prior $n_Q{=}280$
snapshot; context controls pending rerun at $n_Q{=}305$); CLIP's open-set
rate rises from $0.143$ to $0.311$, but erasing the crack costs almost none of it: OSNet retains Rank-1
$0.711$, mAP $0.574$ and open-set $0.393$; CLIP retains Rank-1 $0.600$, mAP
$0.502$, open-set $0.286$. Both erased views still outrank every tight-crop
method: the retrieved cue is the wall, that is, location. Pairwise
$F_1$ moves little with context: all four context variants lie between
$0.346$ and $0.361$, at most $0.056$ above the prior floor, over a change that
gives OSNet $+0.236$ of open-set rate. We read the aggregate as a property
of the data: methods with no shared inductive bias land near the floor, and
wherever a crop method improves, the improvement is not the crack. Ten
methods are a sample, not proof; the result establishes that the obvious
moves buy $0.032$ to $0.069$ above the prior floor on the decision under the
new rerun (floors TBD), reason to change
the formulation, not the encoder.
