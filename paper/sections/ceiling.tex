\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
Ten methods operate on crack-centred crops under a shared validity mask: SIFT/ORB with RANSAC inliers, SuperGlue/LoFTR with pretrained indoor configs, five embeddings plus re-ID OSNet (cosine similarity, all zero-shot on \textsc{CrackID}; YOLOv8 is layer-$9$ \texttt{yolov8n.pt} features). Full input sizes and checkpoints are in code. Context controls use OSNet and CLIP at fixed backbone: \texttt{@ctx1} expands the instance box by $1{\times}$ per side, \texttt{@ctx1@nocrack} inpaints the crack in the same view, isolating wall context. \ours{} registers walls and compares projected masks (Sec.~\ref{sec:method}); a \coverage{} control scores registered pairs constant without inspecting cracks. The synthetic protocol compares OSNet with context against the distinct \hybrid{} configuration. LightGlue and NetVLAD are left as future retrieval baselines.

\subsection{Result: appearance buys almost nothing on the decision}
Table~\ref{tab:main} summarises the primary benchmark ($n_Q{=}280$ answerable test queries over 11 walls; frozen snapshot). Rank-1 spans $0.352$--$0.654$ (ViT-B/16 and OSNet tied best; OSNet best mAP at $0.520$; SuperGlue best crop DIR@FAR$=0.1$ at $0.429$), yet oracle $F_1^{*}$ spans only $0.305$--$0.359$ ($+0.000$ to $+0.054$ over the $0.305$ floor), and validation-calibrated $F_1^{@v}$ spans $0.293$--$0.351$ with two rows below the floor (DeiT, DINOv2): ranking does not transfer to deciding. The post-calibration collapse is consistent with threshold instability on a small wall-clustered sample compounded by a validation-to-test prevalence shift ($0.292{\to}0.180$; floors $0.452{\to}0.305$); the threshold-free check (pairwise AP/AUROC) awaits the score-matrix release (Sec.~\ref{sec:validity}). With $19$ matchable test identities, no fine ranking among crop rows is claimed. All figures are point estimates.

\subsection{Is it the crack, or the wall behind it?}
\label{sec:controls}
Context helps ranking and open-set but erasure keeps most of it: OSNet Rank-1 $0.654{\to}0.775$ ($0.711$ erased), DIR@FAR $0.168{\to}0.404$ ($0.393$ erased); CLIP open-set $0.143{\to}0.311$ ($0.286$ erased). Both erased views still beat every tight crop: the cue is wall location. Pairwise $F_1^{*}$ stays in the narrow band $0.307$--$0.361$ across base, context, and erased views while open-set moves $+0.236$ for OSNet, so (RQ1/RQ2) methods retrieve within-visit observations but reliable automatic identity decisions remain limited; a ranked list may assist an operator while acceptance needs further evidence.
