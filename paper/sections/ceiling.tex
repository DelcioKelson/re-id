\section{The Appearance Ceiling}
\label{sec:ceiling}

\subsection{Setup}
We evaluate ten crop-scope matchers under the identical protocol of
Sec.~\ref{sec:protocol}, on identical inputs, scored by identical code from
saved score matrices. The population is deliberately broad, spanning five
families:

\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
\item \textbf{Handcrafted keypoints}: SIFT~\cite{lowe2004sift},
ORB~\cite{rublee2011orb}. Similarity is the inlier count after ratio test and
RANSAC verification.
\item \textbf{Learned correspondence}: SuperGlue~\cite{sarlin2020superglue},
LoFTR~\cite{sun2021loftr}. Similarity is the confidence-weighted count of
predicted correspondences.
\item \textbf{General visual embeddings}: ViT-B/16~\cite{dosovitskiy2021vit},
DeiT-S~\cite{touvron2021deit}, CLIP ViT-B/32~\cite{radford2021clip}, and a
YOLOv8 detector backbone. Similarity is cosine distance between L2-normalised
features.
\item \textbf{A re-identification architecture}: OSNet~\cite{zhou2019osnet},
trained for instance-level discrimination.
\item \textbf{A purpose-built crack-shape descriptor} (\textsc{CrackShape}), the
strongest crop-scope baseline we could construct \emph{knowing the domain}: it
PCA-aligns crack pixels to remove in-plane rotation, resamples the centreline
into $48$ bins, normalises perpendicular offsets by crack length to remove
scale, and takes the magnitude spectrum of the offset profile --- invariant to
traversal direction --- with scale-free tortuosity, elongation, relative
thickness, and turning-angle spread appended.
\end{itemize}

\textsc{CrackShape} matters to the argument: if the ceiling were an artefact of
generic encoders meeting a specialised structure, a descriptor designed around
crack geometry should escape it. It does not.

\subsection{Result}
Table~\ref{tab:main} reports all ten methods. The pattern is the point.

\noindent\textbf{Pairwise $F_1$ is flat.} Every method lands in
$[0.305, 0.359]$: mean $0.324$, standard deviation $0.023$, coefficient of
variation $0.068$. The span is $0.054$, or $16.7\%$ of the mean. By comparison
Rank-1 spans $0.302$ ($54.6\%$ of its mean, $\mathrm{CV}=0.164$) and
$\mathrm{DIR}@\mathrm{FAR}{=}0.1$ spans $0.396$ ($\mathrm{CV}=0.655$) over the
same ten methods on the same data. Whatever separates these methods, it does not
separate their ability to decide whether two crops show the same crack.

\noindent\textbf{Ranking ability does not transfer to decision ability.} Across
the ten methods, Rank-1 and pairwise $F_1$ are essentially uncorrelated
(Spearman $\rho = 0.018$, $p = 0.96$). OSNet ties for the best Rank-1 ($0.654$)
and sits near the \emph{bottom} of the pairwise band ($0.309$); SuperGlue ranks
sixth on Rank-1 ($0.555$) yet achieves the best open-set rate of any baseline
($0.414$ versus OSNet's $0.168$). A method that orders a gallery well is not
thereby a method that can say \emph{yes, that is the same crack} --- and it is
the second capability an inspection system needs.

\noindent\textbf{Compute buys nothing.} Cost per query spans $2590\times$, from
$0.003$\,s (\textsc{CrackShape}) to $8.33$\,s (LoFTR). Its rank correlation with
Rank-1 is $\rho = 0.018$ ($p = 0.96$) and with pairwise $F_1$ is $\rho = 0.497$
($p = 0.14$, not significant at $n = 10$). LoFTR costs $1300\times$ OSNet per
query and ranks below it on Rank-1, Rank-5, and mAP.

\noindent\textbf{The absolute level is the real finding.} A pairwise $F_1$ near
$0.33$ is not a merely-imperfect system; it is unusable for maintenance
bookkeeping, where a wrong association silently corrupts a defect history. The
ceiling is low as well as flat.

\subsection{Why we read this as a property of the data}
Three observations support a data-level rather than encoder-level explanation.
The band contains methods with no shared inductive bias --- sparse keypoints,
dense attention, ImageNet features, language-image pretraining, a re-ID metric
objective, hand-designed shape statistics --- and a common limitation of such a
population sits more parsimoniously in the signal than in five independent
architectural failures. The domain-specific descriptor lands at the bottom of
the band. And the failure mode is structural: two distinct hairline cracks under
one illumination are routinely more similar than two views of one crack at
different scales.

We state the limits of this inference. Ten methods are a sample, not a proof;
$n{=}10$ makes the correlations descriptive rather than inferential; and a model
trained on a large corpus of crack instances --- which does not exist --- might
yet break the band. What the result establishes is that \emph{the obvious moves
have been tried and land in the same place}, reason enough to change the
formulation rather than the encoder.

\subsection{A designed viewpoint ablation}
\label{sec:synth}
The evidence above is observational: the viewpoint variation is whatever the
capture produced. To test sensitivity by design, we warp each admitted
photograph, its mask, and its label points by a homography with \emph{exactly
known} scale, rotation, and tilt, and query the wall's \emph{unmodified} real
gallery with it. Only the geometry between the views is synthetic; crack
appearance, texture, and sensor noise are real, and the construction is
self-checking --- decomposing the transform recovers the requested parameters to
floating point.

There is almost no response. From the identity transform (SIFT Rank-1 $0.469$,
ORB $0.456$, $n{=}207$), rotating every query by $45^\circ$ costs SIFT $0.051$
and \emph{gains} ORB $0.046$ ($n{=}201$); a $60^\circ$ tilt costs SIFT $0.037$
and gains ORB $0.033$ ($n{=}192$). A $3\times$ scale change moves SIFT
$0.469 \to 0.424$, but is confounded: upscaling pushes labelled points out of
frame, so $n$ falls $207 \to 66$ and the survivors are a more central subset.

We read this as corroboration, not a separate finding. A merely
\emph{viewpoint-limited} method should degrade as the transform grows; these do
not, because at Rank-1 $\approx 0.46$ they already sit near a floor set by crack
self-similarity. Two limits: only the two cheapest baselines were run, and the
transforms are exactly affine by construction, so this says nothing about
projective distortion.
