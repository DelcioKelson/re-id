\title{Benchmark for Crack Re-Identification in Ticket Management Systems}
\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{D\'elcio Ferramenta, C\'atia Loureiro, Ant\'onio Silva, Beatriz Miranda,\\ Paulo Brito, Ricardo Rodrigues, Duarte Fernandes}
\IEEEauthorblockA{DTx CoLAB}}
\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
In a ticket-based maintenance system, each new photograph of a crack should be assigned to the correct ticket, but if the same crack is photographed from a different angle it may be treated as a new crack, thus breaking its historical record and leading to redundant inspection work. We refer to this as crack re-identification (re-ID), which involves determining whether two photographs depict the same crack. This study presents \textsc{CrackID}, a dataset consisting of 140 photographs of 17 walls and containing 212 distinct crack identities, and uses this dataset to assess ten zero-shot, crop-based matchers drawn from four different families under three scenarios: retrieval, thresholded pairwise decisions, and open-set identification with rejection. The ranking quality varies considerably (Rank-1 scores range from 0.370 to 0.613), although the validation-calibrated pairwise decisions only very slightly exceed the best constant baseline ($F_1$ scores of 0.331 to 0.338 compared to 0.305). Experiments involving context and crack erasure demonstrate that cues related to the wall's location are more important than the appearance of the crack. A geometric reference baseline that aligns the texture of crack-free wall sections and then compares the projected crack masks achieves a detection and identification rate (DIR) of 0.768 at a false alarm rate (FAR) of 0.1 on the 48\% of the images that can be aligned, which is twice the DIR of 0.384 obtained by the best crop-based method. These point estimates are based on a small sample of walls, with each wall group being labelled by a single annotator using homography, so the advantage in the open-set setting depends on the source of the annotations, and the evaluation only considers correspondence within a single visit. These results indicate that good retrieval performance by itself does not guarantee reliable association of cracks and show the value of local visual context and geometric alignment in re-identification. By jointly evaluating retrieval, the matching decisions, and the rejection of unknown defects, \textsc{CrackID} provides a foundation for developing more reliable visual association methods to support defect tracking in ticket management systems, with cross-visit evaluation and ticket-level duplicate reduction being future work.
\end{abstract}
