\title{CrackID: A Benchmark for Crack Re-Identification,\\
and the Limits of Appearance Matching}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{D\'elcio Ferramenta, C\'atia Loureiro, Ant\'onio Silva,
Beatriz Miranda, }
\IEEEauthorblockN{Paulo Brito, 
Nuno da Costa, Ricardo Rodrigues, and Duarte Fernandes }
\IEEEauthorblockA{DTx Colab, Campus de Azur\'em, Universidade do Minho,
4800-058 Braga, Portugal}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Maintenance systems turn photographs into tracked defect records:
A crack re-photographed later must match its record or be filed as new.
Crack \emph{detection} is mature, but no benchmark for crack
\emph{re-identification} is known to us. We introduce \textsc{CrackID}: 140 photographs of
17 walls with 212 crack identities, captured in one continuous walk-around per
wall, so it measures within-visit viewpoint and scale robustness, not
cross-visit re-identification. Every metric is reported against its chance
level, with an annotation-quality report ($45\%$ of identities from an
automatic merge). We benchmark ten crop-based matchers from four families.
Ranking metrics separate them (Rank-1 $0.352$--$0.654$; chance $0.336$), but
on the thresholded same-crack decision none exceeds the $0.305$ pairwise-$F_1$
floor by more than $0.054$. Two controls localize the difficulty: adding wall
context raises OSNet's open-set rate from $0.168$ to $0.404$, yet inpainting
the crack out of that view retains $0.393$: context methods retrieve on
location, not crack appearance. A geometric reference baseline that registers
on wall texture with cracks masked, then Chamfer-matches projected crack
masks, reaches $\mathrm{DIR}@\mathrm{FAR}{=}0.1$ of $0.768$ against $0.429$
for the best crop method; a coverage-only control scores $0.000$. It
answers only $48\%$ of pairs, fails on two smooth-plaster walls, and
is entangled with the homography-based provenance of our annotations; on a
synthetic revisit check, OSNet$+$context outperforms it in ranking and
open-set rate. All results are point estimates on $19$ identities across $11$
walls; we claim the pattern, not the digits.
\end{abstract}
