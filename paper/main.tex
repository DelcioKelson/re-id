\title{Benchmark for Crack Re-Identification in Ticket Management Systems}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{D\'elcio Ferramenta, C\'atia Loureiro, Ant\'onio Silva,
Beatriz Miranda, Paulo Brito, Nuno da Costa, Ricardo Rodrigues, and Duarte Fernandes}
\IEEEauthorblockA{DTx Colab, Campus de Azur\'em, Universidade do Minho,
4800-058 Braga, Portugal}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Building ticket management systems must determine whether a newly reported defect corresponds to an existing ticket or requires a new one. Repeated reports of the same crack from different viewpoints fragment history and duplicate work. We study this association as crack re-identification and introduce \textsc{CrackID} (140 photographs, 17 walls, 212 identities in the frozen benchmark snapshot: 6 validation walls and 11 test walls with 19 matchable test identities). We evaluate ten zero-shot crop-based matchers from four families under retrieval, thresholded decisions, and open-set identification. Retrieval varies (Rank-1 $0.370$--$0.613$), but validation-calibrated pairwise decisions cluster in a narrow band just above the best-constant floor ($F_1^{@v}$ $0.331$--$0.338$ against a $0.305$ floor; oracle $F_1^{*}$ $0.337$--$0.374$). Context and crack-erasure controls implicate wall location over crack appearance. A geometric baseline registering wall texture with cracks masked out and matching projected masks reaches $0.768$ detection-and-identification rate at $0.1$ false alarms on $48\%$ of pairs, against $0.384$ for the best crop method, $0.404$ for the best context control, and $0.000$ for a coverage-only control. All figures are point estimates on a small wall-clustered sample with single-annotator homography-propagated labels, so the open-set advantage is provenance-conditioned and ranking is short-baseline within-visit correspondence. This establishes a dataset and benchmark for the visual association step, leaving cross-visit performance and ticket reduction for future work.
\end{abstract}
