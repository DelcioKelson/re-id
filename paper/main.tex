\title{CrackID: A Within-Visit Benchmark for Crack Re-Identification,\\
and the Limits of Appearance Matching}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{D\'elcio Ferramenta, C\'atia Loureiro, Ant\'onio Silva,
Beatriz Miranda, }
\IEEEauthorblockN{Paulo Brito, 
Nuno da Costa, Ricardo Rodrigues, and Duarte Fernandes }
\IEEEauthorblockA{DTx Colab, Campus de Azur\'em, Universidade do Minho,
4800-058 Braga, Portugal}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Maintenance systems turn photographs into tracked defect records:
a re-photographed crack must match its record or be filed as new.
Crack \emph{detection} is mature, but we know of no benchmark for crack
\emph{re-identification}. We introduce \textsc{CrackID}: 140 photographs of
17 walls with 212 crack identities, each wall captured in one continuous
walk-around, so the benchmark measures within-visit viewpoint and scale
robustness, not cross-visit re-identification. Every metric is reported
against its chance level. We benchmark ten zero-shot crop matchers from four
families. Ranking separates them (Rank-1 $0.370$--$0.613$ on the $n_Q{=}305$
rerun; prior chance $0.336$ TBD),
but on the thresholded same-crack decision none exceeds the prior $0.305$
pairwise-$F_1$ floor by more than $0.069$ (new $F_1^{*}$ $0.337$--$0.374$;
floors TBD). Adding wall context raises OSNet's
open-set rate from $0.168$ to $0.404$ (prior snapshot), yet inpainting the crack out retains
$0.393$: context retrieves on location, not crack appearance. A geometric
reference that registers on wall texture with cracks masked and Chamfer-matches
projected masks reaches $\mathrm{DIR}@\mathrm{FAR}{=}0.1$ of $0.768$ against
$0.384$ for the best new crop method (SuperGlue) and $0.000$ for a coverage-only control,
answering $48\%$ of pairs (geometry/coverage prior snapshot, not numerically
comparable to the rerun). It fails on two smooth-plaster walls and shares
homography-based provenance with the annotations, so the advantage is
provenance-conditioned. On a small single-seed synthetic revisit check,
OSNet$+$context outperforms geometry in ranking and open-set rate. All rows
are point estimates on $19$ test identities; we claim the pattern, not the digits.
\end{abstract}
