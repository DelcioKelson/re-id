\title{Benchmark for Crack Re-Identification in Ticket Management Systems}
\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{D\'elcio Ferramenta, C\'atia Loureiro, Ant\'onio Silva, Beatriz Miranda,\\ Paulo Brito, Ricardo Rodrigues, Duarte Fernandes}
\IEEEauthorblockA{DTx CoLAB}}
\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
In a ticket-based maintenance system, each new photograph of a crack should be linked to the correct ticket. However, the same crack photographed from a different angle may be mistaken for a new defect, breaking its history and leading to unnecessary inspection work. We introduce \textsc{CRACKID}, a dataset designed to study this problem of identifying the same physical crack across photographs. The dataset contains 140 photographs from 17 walls, covering 212 distinct crack identities labelled using homography-based annotation. We evaluate ten off-the-shelf image-matching methods across three tasks: finding the correct photograph, deciding whether two photographs show the same crack, and rejecting photographs of previously unseen cracks. Although some methods retrieve the correct crack reasonably well, their decisions about whether two images show the same crack are only slightly better than a simple constant prediction. Experiments with surrounding wall context suggest that much of the matching signal may come not from the crack itself, but from its surroundings, including nearby cracks. A geometric approach that aligns photographs using the surrounding wall surface performs substantially better on the images that can be aligned, highlighting the potential value of geometric information for reliable crack association. These results show that good retrieval performance alone does not guarantee reliable crack association. \textsc{CRACKID} provides a foundation for developing more dependable visual methods for connecting crack photographs across maintenance records, with cross-visit evaluation and ticket-level duplicate reduction as important next steps. 
\end{abstract}
