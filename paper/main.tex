\title{CrackID: A Within-Visit Benchmark for Crack Re-Identification,\\
and the Limits of Appearance Matching}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{D\'elcio Ferramenta, C\'atia Loureiro, Ant\'onio Silva,
Beatriz Miranda, }
\IEEEauthorblockN{Paulo Brito, 
Nuno da Costa, Ricardo Rodrigues, and Duarte Fernandes }
\IEEEauthorblockA{DTx Colab, Campus de Azur\'em, Universidade do Minho,
4800-058 Braga, Portugal}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Ticket management systems turn photographs into tracked defect records:
a re-photographed crack must match its record or be filed as new. This work,
developed within R2UTechnologies, studies within-visit crack re-identification
under viewpoint and scale change. We benchmark ten zero-shot crop matchers from four
families on \textsc{CrackID}, a within-visit wall-crack benchmark. Ranking separates
them (Rank-1 $0.370$--$0.613$; chance $0.336$), but on the thresholded same-crack-or-not decision
that a ticket record requires, all remain within $+0.037$ of the $0.337$ chance floor. Adding wall context
raises OSNet's open-set rate from $0.168$ to $0.404$, yet erasing the crack retains
$0.393$: context retrieves on location, not crack appearance. A geometric reference
that registers on wall texture with cracks masked and Chamfer-matches projected masks
reaches an open-set rate of $0.768$ at $48\%$ coverage, against $0.384$ for the best
crop method; a coverage-only control scores $0.000$. All accuracy rows are point estimates
with no wall-level intervals. Ordering a gallery and deciding
an identity are different capabilities, and the cue that works is location.
\end{abstract}
