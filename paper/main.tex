\title{Benchmark for Crack Re-Identification in Ticket Management Systems}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{D\'elcio Ferramenta, C\'atia Loureiro, Ant\'onio Silva,
Beatriz Miranda, }
\IEEEauthorblockN{Paulo Brito, 
Nuno da Costa, Ricardo Rodrigues, and Duarte Fernandes }
\IEEEauthorblockA{DTx Colab, Campus de Azur\'em, Universidade do Minho,
4800-058 Braga, Portugal}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Building ticket management systems must determine whether a newly reported defect corresponds to an existing ticket or requires a new one. Repeated reports of the same physical incident, such as a crack in a wall photographed from different viewpoints, can generate multiple tickets, fragmenting the incident history and duplicating inspection and repair work. Reliable visual association is therefore essential for linking reports to the correct ticket. We study this association problem through crack re-identification and introduce CRACKID, a dataset containing 140 photographs of 17 walls and 212 crack identities. We evaluate ten zero-shot crop-based matchers from four method families using retrieval, thresholded identity decisions, and open-set identification, with explicit reference levels. Although retrieval performance varies across methods, thresholded same-crack decisions remain close to the best-constant-predictor baseline. Context and crack-erasure controls indicate that wall location contributes more strongly than local crack appearance. A geometric reference baseline registers photographs using wall texture and matches projected crack masks, achieving an open-set identification rate of 0.725 at a false-alarm rate of 0.1 while scoring 48\% of query--gallery pairs. Its limited coverage, failures on smooth walls, and shared annotation provenance constrain this result. These findings establish a dataset and benchmark task for the visual association step needed to consolidate duplicate defect tickets, while leaving real cross-visit performance and end-to-end ticket reduction for future evaluation.
\end{abstract}
